\begin{abstract}
We prove, with every step checked by the Lean~4 kernel against Mathlib, that the \emph{Frohmanian tether
bound}, together with the classical Beale--Kato--Majda continuation hypothesis, implies global smoothness for the
three-dimensional periodic Navier--Stokes equations, and that the tether bound alone implies Beale--Kato--Majda
integrability. The tether bound \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} asserts that for every smooth,
divergence-free, $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic datum $u_0$ there is one ceiling $Y$ with
$\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\le Y$ along every classical periodic solution from $u_0$ on every interval $[0,T)$.
\textbf{Main Theorem} (\lean{clay_B_exact_of_periodic_tether_bound}): if, for every $\nu>0$,
\lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} and the Beale--Kato--Majda continuation hypothesis
\lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation ν} hold, then the formal version \lean{Clay_B_exact} of Fefferman's statement (B)
holds. \textbf{Second theorem} (\lean{periodicBKMIntegrability_of_periodicTetherBound}): the tether bound at a
datum implies Beale--Kato--Majda integrability there, $\int_{[0,T)}\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt\le
\max(Y,0)\max(T_0,0)$ for every solution on $[0,T)$, $T\le T_0$. The converse, BKM integrability $\Rightarrow$
tether bound, is true classical mathematics conditional on three classical pillars (the Beale--Kato--Majda
theorem, uniqueness of classical periodic solutions, long-time decay); it is described in a separate section,
is not stated as a theorem, and is being formalized. Both hypotheses of the Main Theorem are open, and the
tether bound is as strong as statement (B) itself.

Version 0.1.5 also corrects the $\R^3$-class local hypotheses of version 0.1.4, with Lean proofs of both defects:
the real supremum \lean{vorticity_sup_norm} takes the junk value $0$ on unbounded vorticity, so the 0.1.4
ceiling imposed no constraint at such times (now guarded by \lean{BddAbove}); and the 0.1.4
\lean{LocalTetherRate} demanded two-sided differentiability of $\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$ at the right endpoint,
which reads the path beyond the interval: an explicit local solution from $u_0=0$ with
$\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}=t^{-1}$ refutes it, so the 0.1.4 \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem} held vacuously. The
corrected rate uses right derivatives and holds at zero data. Every changed statement is listed.

The library further formalizes the tether $T(F,G)=-\kappa\|\omega\|_{L^2}^2\int\langle\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega,
\Pi_u\,\delta G/\delta\omega\rangle$, a symmetric correction to the Arnold Lie--Poisson bracket, and proves the
negative-feedback condition (C3), the transported form of (C1) under translations, canonicity inside a
restricted kernel class, the corrected weight condition via concentrated divergence-free test fields, an
\emph{obstruction to damping} (every symmetric kernel satisfying the energy degeneracy (C2) has $B(H,M)=0$ for
every $M$), and the decaying shear and ABC flows as exact global periodic solutions for their own data.
\textbf{This work does not prove global regularity of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations and is not a
Clay Millennium Problem claim.} The build (Lean \texttt{v4.30.0-rc1}, Mathlib \texttt{v4.30.0-rc1}, 2797 jobs) contains 782 theorem
constants (690 stated in the sources and 92 auto-generated equation, congruence and injectivity lemmas) and
1101 declarations in 35 modules, with no \texttt{sorry}, no \texttt{native\_decide}, no axiom
declarations, and every declaration depending only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}; this is enforced in continuous integration and reproduced from a fresh clone by
the single command \texttt{bash scripts/verify.sh} (or \texttt{make verify}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}\label{sec:intro}

Arnold's geometric picture of ideal hydrodynamics \cite{Arnold1966,ArnoldKhesin1998} describes the
Euler equations as Hamiltonian motion on coadjoint orbits of the group $\mathrm{SDiff}$ of
volume-preserving diffeomorphisms, with Lie--Poisson bracket
$\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\omega)=\int\omega\cdot(\delta F/\delta\omega\times\delta G/\delta\omega)$ and kinetic-energy
Hamiltonian $H(\omega)=\tfrac12\int|\BS\,\omega|^2$. The \emph{Frohmanian tether} programme adds to this
bracket a symmetric, negative-semidefinite quadratic term $T$ and asks whether $T$ can supply a damping
mechanism for the vorticity sup-norm $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$, which by the Beale--Kato--Majda
criterion \cite{BKM1984} controls continuation of smooth Navier--Stokes solutions.

This paper reports exactly what the accompanying Lean~4 library proves. The Lean statements are the
source of truth: every result below is stated to match a checked Lean declaration, with every hypothesis
made explicit, and the exact Lean text is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:lean}. The main findings are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Main results} (Section~\ref{sec:main}). The Frohmanian tether bound together with periodic BKM
continuation implies the formal statement (B) (Theorem~\ref{thm:mainA}); the tether bound implies BKM
integrability datum by datum (Theorem~\ref{thm:mainB}). The converse direction and its three classical pillars are
described in Section~\ref{sec:converse}. Section~\ref{sec:corr} corrects the local hypotheses of 0.1.4.
\item The tether $T$ satisfies (C3) unconditionally (Section~\ref{sec:tether}). (C2) is not proved. (C1)
with fixed functionals is refuted for $T$; the transported form of (C1) is proved for $T$ under translations
(Section~\ref{sec:c1t}). The identification $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$ assumed for (C2) in earlier versions is false with the
library's definitions; this is proved in Lean (Theorem~\ref{thm:keref}). The hypotheses formerly used for
(C1) were false or unprovable and have been removed (Section~\ref{sec:removed}).
\item \emph{Obstruction to damping.} For every symmetric kernel $B$ satisfying (C2), $B(H,M)=0$ for every
functional $M$; in particular, \emph{if} $T$ satisfied (C2), then $T(M,H)=T(H,M)=0$
(Section~\ref{sec:damping}). Either way, the symmetric design cannot both preserve the Euler vector field
and produce the cubic damping rate on which the programme relied.
\item \emph{Canonicity} holds only inside a restricted class and follows from saturation and polarization
hypotheses; conditions (C1)--(C3) appear in the statement but are not used (Section~\ref{sec:canon}). For $T$
itself, nondegeneracy, polarization on differentiable functionals, saturation, projection-dependence and the
canonical density are proved; unrestricted polarization and the pointwise weight hypothesis are refuted; the
corrected a.e.\ weight condition is proved (Section~\ref{sec:testfields}).
\item The analytic layer consists of proved real-variable lemmas and \emph{conditional} implications.
Their remaining hypotheses are of two kinds (Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}): classical theorems that are
not in Mathlib (Kato local existence, Beale--Kato--Majda continuation, uniqueness), and hypotheses that are
as strong as the open problem itself (a cubic rate or a uniform vorticity bound for all data)
(Sections~\ref{sec:analytic}--\ref{sec:clay}).
\item \emph{Explicit solutions} (Section~\ref{sec:explicit}). The decaying shear and ABC flows are proved to be
nonzero exact solutions of the library's equations and, for $k=2\pi m$, global smooth periodic solutions for
their own data. No nonzero periodic field is a Kato witness (infinite energy on $\R^3$), so the $\R^3$ chain
cannot be instantiated on them; this is proved, not assumed.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope statement.} No result in this paper or in the library asserts global existence,
smoothness or uniqueness of solutions of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations. Every theorem whose
conclusion mentions global solutions is an implication from hypotheses that are open. Section~\ref{sec:does}
lists precisely what the Lean development does and does not prove.

\section{Main results of version 0.1.5}\label{sec:main}

Throughout, $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$ is \lean{vorticity_sup_norm (vorticity (u t))}, and
\lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolutionOn ν T u₀ u p} is the class of classical solutions on $[0,T)$: $(u,p)$ jointly
$C^\infty$ on $[0,T)\times\R^3$, the equations with one-sided time derivative and zero forcing,
$\operatorname{div}u=0$, $u(0)=u_0$, and $u(t),p(t)$ $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic (exact text in
Appendix~\ref{app:lean:clay}).

\begin{definition}[The Frohmanian tether bound]\label{def:ptb}
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{TetherImpliesBKM.PeriodicTetherBound}.\par\noindent
For $\nu\in\R$ and a field $u_0$, \lean{PeriodicTetherBound ν u₀} asserts that there is $Y\in\R$ such that
$M(t)\le Y$ for every $T$, every classical periodic solution $(u,p)$ from $u_0$ on $[0,T)$ and every
$t\in[0,T)$. The \emph{closing bound} \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} asserts \lean{PeriodicTetherBound ν u₀}
for every smooth divergence-free $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic $u_0$; this is a definitional identity
(\lean{periodicTetherClosingBound_iff}, proved by \lean{Iff.rfl}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Periodic Beale--Kato--Majda integrability]
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{TetherImpliesBKM.PeriodicBKMIntegrability}.\par\noindent
\lean{PeriodicBKMIntegrability ν u₀} asserts: for every horizon $T_0\in\R$ there is $C\in[0,\infty)$ such
that
\[
\int_{[0,T)} M(t)\,dt\;\le\;C
\]
for every $T\le T_0$ and every classical periodic solution from $u_0$ on $[0,T)$. The integral is the lower
Lebesgue integral $\int^{-}_{[0,T)}\mathrm{ofReal}(M(t))\,dt\in[0,\infty]$ (Mathlib's \lean{lintegral} with
\lean{ENNReal.ofReal}), so no measurability side condition is involved.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem, kernel-checked]\label{thm:mainA}
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{ClayStatements.clay_B_exact_of_periodic_tether_bound}.\par\noindent
Assume, for every $\nu>0$,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} (the Frohmanian tether bound, Definition~\ref{def:ptb}), and
\item \lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation ν}: for every smooth divergence-free $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic $u_0$, a single
ceiling $Y$ for $M$ along all classical periodic solutions from $u_0$ on all intervals $[0,T)$ yields a global
solution $(u,p)$ satisfying \lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolution ν u₀ (fun _ _ => 0) u p}.
\end{enumerate}
Then \lean{Clay_B_exact} holds: for every $\nu>0$ and every smooth, divergence-free, $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic
$u_0$ there exist $(u,p)$, jointly $C^\infty$ on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, solving the Navier--Stokes equations
with zero forcing, with $u(0)=u_0$ and $u(t),p(t)$ periodic for all $t\ge0$.
\end{theorem}

Hypothesis (ii) packages three classical results that are not formalized: local existence of classical
periodic solutions, their uniqueness, and the continuation of a solution whose vorticity stays bounded
(Beale--Kato--Majda \cite{BKM1984}). Its antecedent is literally the body of (i); the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:mainA} is therefore the composition of (ii) with (i), and the analytic content lies entirely in
the two hypotheses. Hypothesis (i) is the key estimate. It is open; it is not supplied by the tether kernel of
Sections~\ref{sec:tether}--\ref{sec:damping}; and it is as strong as statement (B) itself (for $\nu>0$, global
smoothness on $\T$ together with the decay of the energy gives a time-uniform bound on $M$).

\begin{theorem}[Tether bound $\Rightarrow$ BKM integrability, kernel-checked]\label{thm:mainB}
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{TetherImpliesBKM.periodicBKMIntegrability_of_periodicTetherBound}.\par\noindent
For every $\nu\in\R$ and every field $u_0$, \lean{PeriodicTetherBound ν u₀} implies
\lean{PeriodicBKMIntegrability ν u₀}. Explicitly, if $M\le Y$ along every classical periodic solution from
$u_0$, then for every $T_0$ the constant $C=\max(Y,0)\cdot\max(T_0,0)$ bounds $\int_{[0,T)}M(t)\,dt$ for every
such solution on $[0,T)$ with $T\le T_0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $t\in[0,T)$, $\mathrm{ofReal}(M(t))\le\mathrm{ofReal}(\max(Y,0))$, so by monotonicity of the lower
integral on the measurable set $[0,T)$ (\lean{setLIntegral_mono'}), \lean{setLIntegral_const} and
\lean{Real.volume_Ico}, the integral is at most $\mathrm{ofReal}(\max(Y,0))\cdot\mathrm{ofReal}(T)\le
\mathrm{ofReal}(\max(Y,0)\max(T_0,0))<\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[kernel-checked]
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{TetherImpliesBKM.periodicBKMIntegrability_of_periodicTetherClosingBound}.\par\noindent
\lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} implies \lean{PeriodicBKMIntegrability ν u₀} for every smooth
divergence-free $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic $u_0$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The converse direction: status (not a theorem of this paper)}\label{sec:converse}
For $\nu>0$ and a fixed smooth divergence-free periodic $u_0$, the converse implication
\lean{PeriodicBKMIntegrability ν u₀} $\Rightarrow$ \lean{PeriodicTetherBound ν u₀} is true classical mathematics,
by the following route; it is \emph{not} proved in Lean, and no statement of this paper asserts it.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(P\arabic*)]
\item \emph{Beale--Kato--Majda continuation} \cite{BKM1984}: if $\int_0^{T}M\,dt<\infty$ along the maximal
classical solution, the solution extends beyond $T$; with BKM integrability on every horizon the solution is
global.
\item \emph{Uniqueness of classical periodic solutions} (energy estimate for the difference and Gronwall), so
that every classical solution on $[0,T)$ is the restriction of the global one.
\item \emph{Long-time decay}: by the energy inequality $\int_0^\infty\|\nabla u\|_{L^2}^2\,dt<\infty$, so
$\|\nabla u(t_0)\|_{L^2}$ is small at some $t_0$; small-data global theory and parabolic smoothing then give
$\sup_{t\ge t_0+1}M(t)<\infty$, and continuity bounds $M$ on $[0,t_0+1]$. (A nonzero mean velocity is removed by
a Galilean change of frame, which does not change $\omega$.)
\end{enumerate}
These three pillars are the formalization targets of the follow-up work; (P2) is the first, and it also discharges
the uniqueness input contained in \lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation}. With (P1)--(P3) the two bounds are equivalent
datum by datum. Over all data the two statements are each classically equivalent to statement (B), so the equivalence
locates the tether bound exactly at Beale--Kato--Majda strength; it does not reduce the open problem.

\paragraph{Boundedness of $\omega(t)$ in the periodic class.} For $t\in[0,T)$ a classical periodic solution has
$\omega(t)$ continuous and periodic, hence bounded, so the real supremum \lean{vorticity_sup_norm} takes its genuine
value there. This classical fact is not formalized and is used by no theorem.

\section{Corrections in version 0.1.5: the local tether hypotheses}\label{sec:corr}

The definitions of the $\R^3$-class local hypotheses in version 0.1.4 had two defects, found by a definition
audit and now proved in Lean (module \lean{TetherDefinitionAudit}, which keeps the 0.1.4 definitions verbatim
as \lean{LocalTetherBoundV014} and \lean{LocalTetherRateV014}). Both are corrected; the exact statement changes
are listed in Section~\ref{sec:diff}.

\subsection{The junk value of the supremum}
\lean{vorticity_sup_norm ω} is the real supremum $\bigsqcup_x\|\omega(x)\|$, which Mathlib defines to be $0$
when the range is not bounded above.

\begin{theorem}[kernel-checked]
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{vorticity_sup_norm_eq_zero_of_not_bddAbove}, \leanbrk{localTetherBoundV014_constraint_free_of_unbounded}.\par\noindent
If $x\mapsto\|\omega(x)\|$ is unbounded then \lean{vorticity_sup_norm ω} $=0$. Consequently, at every time $t$
at which $\omega(t)$ is unbounded, the 0.1.4 ceiling $M(t)\le Y$ holds for every $Y\ge0$.
\end{theorem}

So the 0.1.4 \lean{LocalTetherBound} and \lean{LocalFiniteTimeVorticityBound} imposed no constraint at such
times. Smooth divergence-free finite-energy data with unbounded vorticity exist: with a bump $\psi\in C_c^\infty$
equal to $1$ near $0$ and centres $c_n$ at mutual distance larger than twice the diameter of its support, let
$A=\sum_n 8^{-n}\sin(4^nx_1)\,\psi(x-c_n)\,e_3$ and $u_0=\nabla\times A$. The sum is locally finite, so $u_0$ is
smooth and divergence-free; $\|u_0\|_{L^2}^2\lesssim\sum_n(8^{-n}4^n)^2<\infty$; and near $c_n$,
$\omega_0=\nabla\times\nabla\times A=-\Delta A=8^{-n}16^n\sin(4^nx_1)\,e_3$ reaches size $2^n$. (This
construction is classical and not formalized.) Every such datum admits a local physical solution on the
degenerate interval $[0,0]$:

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{exists_isLocalPhysicalSolution_zero_time}]
For every $\nu$ and every smooth divergence-free $u_0$ of finite energy, the affine path
$u(t)=u_0+t\,v_0$ with $v_0=\nu\Delta u_0-(u_0\cdot\nabla)u_0$ and zero pressure satisfies
\lean{IsLocalPhysicalSolution ν 0 u₀ u (fun _ => 0)}.
\end{theorem}

The 0.1.5 definitions add the clause \lean{BddAbove (Set.range fun x => ‖vorticity (u t) x‖)} at every
$t\in[0,T]$. With it, data with unbounded initial vorticity never satisfy the bound
(\lean{not_localTetherBound_of_unbounded}, \lean{not_localTetherRate_of_unbounded}); accordingly the closing bound
\lean{LocalTetherClosingBound} and the theorems that consume a bound for all data now range over data with
bounded initial vorticity. Whether the 0.1.4 forms were satisfiable by the junk value alone at some datum (which
would require unbounded vorticity along every local solution at every time) is a question about solutions on
$\R^3$ that is not settled here; the guard removes the possibility.

\subsection{Two-sided differentiability at the endpoint}
The 0.1.4 \lean{LocalTetherRate} required \lean{DifferentiableAt ℝ M s} at every $s\in[0,T]$. At $s=T$ this
depends on the path for $t>T$, where a local solution on $[0,T]$ is unconstrained.

\begin{theorem}[kernel-checked]\label{thm:escape}
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{isLocalPhysicalSolution_escapePath}, \leanbrk{vorticity_sup_norm_escapePath_pos}, \leanbrk{not_localTetherRateV014_zero}, \leanbrk{not_forall_localTetherRateV014}.\par\noindent
Let $u(t)=0$ for $t\le0$ and $u(t)=t^2\,(\sin(t^{-3}x_2),\cos(t^{-3}x_2),0)$ for $t>0$ (\lean{escapePath}). Then $u$
is a local physical solution on $[0,0]$ from $u_0=0$ with zero pressure, and $M(t)=t^{-1}$ for $t>0$ while
$M(0)=0$. Hence $M$ is not differentiable at $0$, \lean{LocalTetherRateV014 0 ν} is false for every $\nu$, and
$\forall u_0\,\nu$, \lean{LocalTetherRateV014 u₀ ν} is false.
\end{theorem}

The last statement is the hypothesis \texttt{hDI} of the 0.1.4 \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem}. \textbf{The
0.1.4 \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem} was therefore vacuously true.} The 0.1.5 \lean{LocalTetherRate} asks for
continuity of $M$ on $[0,T]$, a right derivative $d\le CM(s)^2-\kappa''M(s)^3$
(\lean{HasDerivWithinAt M d (Set.Ici s) s}) at every $s\in[0,T)$, and the \lean{BddAbove} clause; it reads $u$ on
$[0,T]$ only, and it holds at $u_0=0$ (\lean{localTetherRate_zero}). The comparison step uses the new
\lean{comparison_ode_stability_Icc_right}, proved from Mathlib's
\lean{image_le_of_liminf_slope_right_lt_deriv_boundary} and \lean{HasDerivWithinAt.liminf_right_slope_le}.

\begin{remark}[The global-path rate; not formalized]
\lean{FrohmanianTetherRate} (global solutions, auxiliary lemmas only; unchanged in 0.1.5) also asks for two-sided
differentiability of $M$ at $s=0$, where \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} continues $u$ affinely,
$u(t)=u_0+t\,\partial_tu(0^+)$ for $t<0$. If $\|\omega_0\|$ attains its maximum $M_0>0$ at two points $a,b$ with
$\langle\xi,\partial_t\omega(0^+)\rangle$ equal to $\alpha$ at $a$ and $\beta\neq\alpha$ at $b$ ($\xi=\omega_0/M_0$),
then $M(t)\ge M_0+t\max(\alpha,\beta)+o(t)$ for $t\downarrow0$ and $M(t)\ge M_0+t\min(\alpha,\beta)+O(t^2)$ for
$t\uparrow0$, which is incompatible with differentiability at $0$. Thus \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate} fails at any datum
carrying a global physical solution with such a pair of maxima. No nonzero global finite-energy solution is
formalized, so this is recorded as a mathematical remark.
\end{remark}

\section{The formal setting}\label{sec:setting}

We record the definitions as formalized, including the points at which they differ from the classical
objects.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Space.} \lean{T3} is \lean{EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)}, i.e.\ $\R^3$ with Lebesgue measure,
\emph{not} the quotient torus $\R^3/\mathbb{Z}^3$. Periodicity, where needed (Section~\ref{sec:clay}), is
the predicate \lean{IsZ3Periodic} on functions on $\R^3$. The notation $\mathrm{SDiff}(\T)$ in the title
refers to the motivating geometry; no diffeomorphism group of $\T$ is constructed.
\item \textbf{Phase space.} \lean{CoadjointOrbit} is the type of pointwise divergence-free vector fields
$\omega:\R^3\to\R^3$ (a subtype; no orbit structure is built in).
\item \textbf{Functional derivative.} Let $L^2$ be \lean{Lp (EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)) 2 volume}. A
\emph{test direction} (\lean{IsTestDirection}) is a $C^\infty$, compactly supported field in
\lean{CoadjointOrbit} (hence divergence-free); \lean{DivFreeL2} is the closure in $L^2$ of the span of the
test directions. A vorticity is \emph{regular} (\lean{IsRegularVorticity}) if each component is
differentiable everywhere. \lean{IsFunctionalGradient F ω g} says: $g\in$ \lean{DivFreeL2}, $\omega$ is
regular, and $\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}F(\omega+t\varepsilon)=\langle g,\varepsilon\rangle_{L^2}$ for every test
direction $\varepsilon$. Such a $g$ is unique (\lean{isFunctionalGradient_unique}: the difference of two
gradients is orthogonal to a dense subset of \lean{DivFreeL2}). \lean{FunctionalDerivative F ω} is (a
representative of) this unique $g$ when it exists, and $0$ otherwise; in particular it is $0$ at every
non-regular $\omega$ (\lean{functionalDerivative_eq_zero_of_not_regular}). The value is determined up to a
null set, and no arbitrary choice among different derivatives is made. Earlier versions chose a Gâteaux
representative, which is never unique (Theorem~\ref{thm:nouniq}); the predicate \lean{IsGateauxRepresentative}
is kept only for that record.
\item \textbf{Velocity and energy.} \lean{velocity_from_vorticity ω} is the Biot--Savart integral
\lean{BiotSavart ω.val}, and \lean{KineticEnergyHamiltonian ω} $=\tfrac12\int\|\BS\,\omega\|^2$.
\item \textbf{Navier--Stokes.} \lean{navier_stokes_eq u p ν} (and \lean{NS_PDE}) asserts the momentum
equation with Mathlib's total derivative \lean{deriv} in time, and $\nabla\cdot u=0$, pointwise for $t\ge0$.
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution u} adds pointwise time-differentiability for $t\ge0$, spatial continuity,
finite kinetic energy, the energy inequality $\int\|u(t)\|^2\le\int\|u(0)\|^2$, and an \emph{affine
extension to negative times}: there is a field $v_0$ with $u(t)=u(0)+t\,v_0$ for all $t\le0$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Repair of \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution}.} Earlier versions required $u(t)=u(0)$ for $t\le0$. Since
\lean{deriv} is the two-sided derivative, differentiability at $t=0$ then forced $\partial_tu(0)=0$, so a
solution whose velocity changes at the initial time could never qualify; with the energy inequality, this
made the class essentially one of solutions that are stationary at $t=0$. The negative-time clause exists
only to stop junk values at $t<0$ from interacting with \lean{deriv} at $0$; the affine extension does this
and allows any initial time derivative $v_0$. The sanity lemma
\lean{PhysicalSolutionSanity.exists_physical_time_deriv_ne_zero} exhibits a path $u$ satisfying
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} whose time derivative at $t=0$ is nonzero at some point
(\lean{not_forall_physical_time_deriv_zero} states the same as the failure of the old consequence). The path
is $g(t)\,\varphi$ with $\varphi(x)=\max(0,1-\|x\|)\,e_0$, $g(t)=1-t$ for $t\le0$ and $g(t)=1/(1+t)$ for $t>0$;
it is \emph{not} a Navier--Stokes solution. The library's nonzero Navier--Stokes solutions (the constant flows
and the shear and ABC flows of Section~\ref{sec:explicit}) all have infinite energy on $\R^3$ and so are not in this
class; non-vacuity of the class for genuine non-steady flows is not formalized.
The exact definitions are quoted in Appendix~\ref{app:lean:setting}.

\section{The tether kernel and conditions (C1)--(C3)}\label{sec:tether}

\begin{definition}[\leanbrk{Pi_u}, \leanbrk{TetherKernel}, \leanbrk{TetheredBracket}]
For velocity fields $u,v$ put
$\Pi_u v=v-\bigl(\int\langle v,u\rangle/\int\|u\|^2\bigr)\,u$ (in Lean, division by $0$ is $0$, so $\Pi_u v=v$
when $\int\|u\|^2=0$). With $\kappa=3/2$ (\lean{kappa_eq_three_halves}),
$\mathcal E(\omega)=\int\|\omega\|^2$ (\lean{Enstrophy}) and $u=\BS\,\omega$,
\[
T(\omega;F,G)=-\kappa\,\mathcal E(\omega)\int\bigl\langle\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega,\;\Pi_u\,\delta G/\delta\omega\bigr\rangle\,dx,
\qquad \{F,G\}_{\Tf}=\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}+T(F,G).
\]
\end{definition}

The three conditions are predicates on a kernel $B(\omega;F,G)$:
(C1) \lean{InvariantUnderCoadjointAction}: $B(g\cdot\omega;F,G)=B(\omega;F,G)$ for all $g$ in a group
\lean{SDiff} acting on \lean{CoadjointOrbit};
(C2) \lean{DegenerateWRTKineticEnergy}: $B(\omega;F,H)=0$ for all $F,\omega$;
(C3) \lean{ProducesControllableNegativeFeedback}:
$B(\omega;F,F)\le-\kappa\,\mathcal E(\omega)\int\|\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega\|^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Symmetry and (C3); \leanbrk{tetherKernel_comm}, \leanbrk{tetherKernel_C3}]
For all $\omega,F,G$: $T(\omega;F,G)=T(\omega;G,F)$, and $T$ satisfies (C3). No hypotheses.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{(C2) is not proved.} No theorem of the library asserts that $T$ satisfies (C2). The only (C2)
fact proved for $T$ is the degenerate case \lean{tetherKernel_C2_of_no_gateaux}: if $H$ has no functional
gradient at $\omega$ (for example, $\omega$ not regular), then $T(\omega;F,H)=0$ for every $F$ (because the
derivative is then $0$). At regular $\omega$, (C2) would need $\delta H/\delta\omega$, classically the Leray
part of the stream function; the Biot--Savart operator is unbounded on $L^2(\R^3)$, this is not in Mathlib, and
(C2) is expected to fail there (see below). Section~\ref{sec:ke} explains why the former route to (C2) was removed.

\paragraph{(C1) with fixed functionals is refuted for $T$ under the concrete action.} Earlier versions derived (C1) for $T$ from two hypotheses,
\lean{SDiffActionHyp} with a transitivity field and \lean{TetherIntegralPreserved}; both are removed
(Section~\ref{sec:removed}) and no (C1) theorem for $T$ remains. \lean{SDiffActionHyp} now has the single
field \lean{casimir_invariant} (every Casimir $\int f(|\omega|)$ is invariant under the action). For the
concrete group \lean{ConcreteSDiff.SDiffConcrete} of measure-preserving, measurable, divergence-preserving
permutations of $\R^3$ acting by $g\cdot\omega=\omega\circ g^{-1}$ (scalar composition, not the geometric
push-forward $(Dg\,\omega)\circ g^{-1}$), the structure is proved outright (\lean{ConcreteSDiff.sdiffActionHyp}).

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{not_tetherKernel_invariant_concrete}]\label{thm:c1ref}
$T$ does not satisfy (C1) for the concrete group: \lean{¬ InvariantUnderCoadjointAction ConcreteSDiff.G TetherKernel}.
\end{theorem}
The translations $x\mapsto x+h$ belong to the concrete group (\lean{ConcreteSDiff.translate}). Take $g$ the
translation by $3e_0$, $\omega=\rho_0$ and $g\cdot\omega=\rho_{3e_0}$ (rotation fields, see
Section~\ref{sec:canon}), and a rotation field $\varphi\in\{\rho_0,\rho_{3e_0}\}$ with
$T(\omega;F_\varphi,F_\varphi)\neq0$ (\lean{tetherKernel_rot_nondegenerate}). The functional
$F(\eta)=|F_\varphi(\eta)-F_\varphi(g\cdot\omega)|$ has no gradient at $g\cdot\omega$, so $T(g\cdot\omega;F,F)=0$; since
$F_\varphi(\omega)\neq F_\varphi(g\cdot\omega)$, at $\omega$ it has gradient $\pm\varphi$ and
$T(\omega;F,F)=T(\omega;F_\varphi,F_\varphi)\neq0$. The predicate \lean{InvariantUnderCoadjointAction} keeps $F,G$
fixed while $\omega$ moves, which is not the physically meaningful notion: the transported form, with $F,G$ moved
along with $\omega$, is proved for translations in Section~\ref{sec:c1t}.

\subsection{Transported (equivariant) (C1)}\label{sec:c1t}
\begin{definition}[\leanbrk{InvariantUnderTransportedCoadjointAction}]
For a group $S$ acting on \lean{CoadjointOrbit}, a kernel $B$ is \emph{transport-invariant} if
$B(g\cdot\omega;\,g_*F,\,g_*G)=B(\omega;F,G)$ for all $g,F,G,\omega$, where $(g_*F)(\eta)=F(g^{-1}\cdot\eta)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{tetherKernel_transported_invariant}]\label{thm:c1t}
$T$ is transport-invariant for the translation subgroup \lean{ConcreteSDiff.transSubgroup} of the concrete
group (the elements \lean{translate h}, $x\mapsto x+h$, with $(\tau_h\cdot\omega)(x)=\omega(x-h)$,
\lean{translate_smul_val}).
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof (formalized).} Let $L_h$ be translation on $L^2$, $L_hv=v(\cdot-h)$; in Lean it is Mathlib's
\lean{Lp.compMeasurePreservingₗᵢ} along the measure-preserving map $x\mapsto x-h$
(\lean{measurePreserving_sub_right}), a linear isometry. (1) $L_h$ maps each test field to a test field and so,
being continuous, maps \lean{DivFreeL2} (closure of their span) into itself (\lean{l2Translate_mem_divFreeL2},
Mathlib \lean{map_mem_closure}). (2) If $g$ is the functional gradient of $F$ at $\omega$, then $L_hg$ is the
gradient of $\tau_{h*}F$ at $\tau_h\cdot\omega$ (\lean{isFunctionalGradient_translate}): the line
$\tau_h^{-1}\cdot(\tau_h\omega+t\varepsilon)$ is $\omega+t\,\tau_h^{-1}\varepsilon$, and
$\langle g,\tau_h^{-1}\varepsilon\rangle=\langle L_hg,\varepsilon\rangle$ (\lean{LinearIsometry.inner_map_map}).
Applying this to $-h$ gives the converse, so $\delta(\tau_{h*}F)/\delta\omega(\tau_h\omega)=(\delta F/\delta\omega(\omega))(\cdot-h)$
a.e.\ in all cases (\lean{functionalDerivative_translate}). (3) The Biot--Savart integral commutes with
translations, $\BS(\tau_h\omega)=(\BS\,\omega)(\cdot-h)$ (\lean{biotSavart_translate}; Mathlib
\lean{integral_add_right_eq_self}), hence so do the Gram--Schmidt coefficient of $\Pi_u$ and the projected
pairing (\lean{tI_translate}, Mathlib \lean{integral_sub_right_eq_self}). (4) The enstrophy is invariant
(\lean{ConcreteSDiff.enstrophy_invariant}).

\paragraph{Sanity.} The property is not vacuous: the point-evaluation kernel $B(\omega;F,G)=|\omega(e_0/2)|$
fails it (\lean{not_transported_invariant_pointEval}). It differs from the fixed-functional (C1): on the same
translation subgroup the latter fails for $T$ (\lean{not_tetherKernel_invariant_transSubgroup}, via the generic
\lean{not_tetherKernel_invariant_of_shift}), while the transported form holds.

\paragraph{Open: rotations and general diffeomorphisms.} The concrete group acts by scalar composition
$\omega\circ g^{-1}$, which is the correct transport of a vorticity only for translations; for a rotation $R$
the geometric action is $R\,\omega(R^{-1}x)$ and for a volume-preserving diffeomorphism $\varphi$ it is the
push-forward $(D\varphi\,\omega)\circ\varphi^{-1}$. Neither action is formalized, and transport-invariance of $T$
under them is open. Under the full concrete group (which contains non-smooth measure-preserving maps) one
expects transport-invariance to fail, since such maps destroy regularity and hence gradients.

\subsection{The identification \texorpdfstring{$\delta H/\delta\omega=u$}{dH/dw = u} is false (removed)}\label{sec:ke}

Earlier versions used the named proposition \lean{PlanSteps.KineticEnergyDerivIsVelocity}:
\emph{for every $\omega$, \lean{FunctionalDerivative KineticEnergyHamiltonian ω = velocity_from_vorticity ω}},
as the hypothesis of (C2) for $T$. It has been deleted from the library, together with every theorem that
assumed it or its pointwise form, because it is false.

\begin{theorem}[Refutation of $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$]\label{thm:keref}
(Lean: \lean{KineticEnergyDerivative.}\allowbreak\lean{not_forall_functionalDerivative_kineticEnergy_eq_velocity}.)
It is not the case that \lean{FunctionalDerivative KineticEnergyHamiltonian ω = velocity_from_vorticity ω}
for every \lean{ω : CoadjointOrbit}.
\end{theorem}

\emph{Proof idea (formalized).} Let $B$ be the open unit ball centred at $(0,2,0)$ and
$\omega=\mathbf 1_B\,e_1$. Each coordinate of $\omega$ takes two values, so its Fréchet
derivative is $0$ everywhere (Mathlib's \lean{fderiv} is $0$ at non-differentiable points, and a
two-valued function differentiable at a point is locally constant there); hence $\nabla\cdot\omega=0$ in
the library's sense and $\omega$ is a point of \lean{CoadjointOrbit}. It is not regular
(the first coordinate jumps at $(1,2,0)$), so \lean{FunctionalDerivative} is $0$ at $\omega$. But the third
component of $\BS\,\omega$ at the origin is $-(4\pi)^{-1}\int_B y_2\,|y|^{-3}\,dy<0$, so $\BS\,\omega\neq0$.
(Under the former choice-based encoding the argument compared $\omega$ with $2\omega$.)
Theorem~\ref{thm:keref} uses only the three standard axioms.

\begin{theorem}[Non-uniqueness of Gâteaux representatives]\label{thm:nouniq}
(Lean: \lean{KineticEnergyDerivative.not_unique_isGateauxRepresentative},
\lean{KineticEnergyDerivative.not_unique_velocity_representative}.)
If $\delta$ is a Gâteaux representative of $F$ at $\omega$, so is a field $\delta'\neq\delta$ (change $\delta$ at
the origin). Consequently, for no $\omega$ is $\BS\,\omega$ the \emph{unique} Gâteaux representative of $H$
at $\omega$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Scope of the refutation.} The counterexample is a non-smooth vorticity field, at which the
derivative is $0$ by definition; it shows that the Lean hypothesis, as stated, is false,
and therefore that every theorem which assumed it was vacuous. It does not by itself settle what the
derivative is at smooth fields. For the $L^2$ vorticity pairing used by \lean{IsFunctionalGradient}, the
classical formal computation gives $\delta H/\delta\omega=\BS^*\BS\,\omega$, the vector stream function
$\psi$ with $\nabla\times\psi=u$, not $u$; this identity is \emph{not} formalized. What is formalized is the
velocity-pairing statement \lean{isVelocityGateauxRepresentative_kineticEnergy_at}: with the pairing
$\int\langle\delta,\BS\,\varepsilon\rangle$, the derivative of $H$ along $\varepsilon$ is represented by $u$
(under explicit integrability hypotheses). Whether $T$, with $\psi$ in place of $u$, satisfies (C2) is open
and in general expected to fail: $\Pi_u\psi=0$ would require $\psi$ to be parallel to $u$.

\subsection{Other removed hypotheses (version 0.1.2)}\label{sec:removed}

Each item below was an explicit hypothesis of earlier versions. Each was false, unprovable in the encoding,
or vacuous, and has been deleted together with every theorem that used it. No remaining statement was
weakened silently: a theorem whose proof needed a removed hypothesis was deleted, not re-proved under a
weaker one. All refutations use only the three standard axioms.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Transitivity on Casimir levels} (field \lean{transitive_on_casimir_levels} of
\lean{SDiffActionHyp}: fields with equal Casimirs lie in one orbit). \emph{Refuted} for the concrete action:
\lean{ConcreteSDiff.not_transitive_on_casimir_levels}. The constant fields $e_0$ and $e_1$ have the same
Casimirs but different orbits, because the action fixes every constant field. Deleted with it:
\lean{orbit_eq_casimir_level}.
\item \textbf{\lean{TetherIntegralPreserved}} (invariance of $\int\|\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega\|^2$ under the
action). It concerned the unconstrained choice made by the former \lean{FunctionalDerivative}, so it could be
neither proved nor refuted from the definitions. It was not reinstated after the rebuild. Deleted with it: \lean{tether_coadjoint_invariance},
\lean{tether_satisfies_C1}, \lean{tetherKernel_C1}, \lean{Combined.tetherKernel_C1_of_casimir},
\lean{Combined.tetherKernel_C1_of_volumePreserving} (whose structure field carried the same assumption),
\lean{Combined.combined_conditional_capstone}, and related bridges.
\item \textbf{Pointwise strain bound} \lean{PointwiseStrainSupBound}: $\|\nabla u(x)\|\le C\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$.
\emph{Refuted}: \lean{StrainBoundRefutation.not_forall_strain_le_sup_vorticity}. For every $C$, the linear
strain field $u(y)=(y_1,y_0,0)$ has $\nabla\times u=0$ and $\nabla u\neq0$. (Classically the bound fails even
for smooth decaying fields, since the Calder\'on--Zygmund operator is unbounded on $L^\infty$.) Deleted with
it: the stretching estimates at a spatial maximum (\lean{stretching_inner_le_at_max} and relatives),
\lean{tethered_stretching_of_weight}, \lean{reduced_pairing_of_unmodified_and_tether} and the other
conditional bridges built on them.
\item \textbf{Circular Jacobi lemma.} \lean{tethered_jacobi_identity} assumed that the Jacobiator equals a
cyclic integral (\texttt{hident}) and that this integral vanishes (\texttt{hvanish}), i.e.\ its conclusion.
Deleted. No Jacobi identity is claimed for $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Tf}$.
\item \textbf{Antisymmetric uniqueness clause.} The former first clause of \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem}
(and \lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether_antisymmetric}) quantified over antisymmetric kernels that saturate
$T$. Proved: \lean{antisymmetric_clause_forces_zero}. Antisymmetry, saturation and the two polarization
hypotheses force $B=0$ \emph{and} $T=0$, so the clause applied to no kernel unless $T\equiv0$. Deleted.
\item \textbf{Uniform lower bound \texttt{hTlb} on local existence times}, with the restart hypotheses
\texttt{hInt}, \texttt{huniq\_shift} of \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem}. A data-independent lower bound on
Kato existence times for all smooth finite-energy data is not known. By Navier--Stokes scaling it is of the
same strength as the open problem; it is not known to be false. It was removed and the theorem re-proved
through the BKM route (Theorem~\ref{thm:fft}).
\item \textbf{Unrestricted polarization of $T$} (\lean{Polarizes TetherKernel}). \emph{Refuted}:
\lean{TetherNondegeneracy.not_tetherKernel_polarizes}. The functional $F(\omega)=|\int\langle\omega,\varphi\rangle|$
(\lean{absFun}) has no gradient at $\omega=0$, so its derivative is $0$ there, while $F+G$ for a suitable
linear $G$ has one; the identity fails. It is replaced by \lean{PolarizesOnDifferentiable}
(polarization for functionals that have gradients), which is \emph{proved} for $T$
(\lean{tetherKernel_polarizesOnDifferentiable}).
\item \textbf{Pointwise weight hypothesis} \texttt{h\_weight} (every density equals $-c\,\mathcal E(\omega)$ at
\emph{every} $x$). \emph{Refuted} for every kernel that has a density
(\lean{not_pointwiseEnstrophyWeight_of_density}): changing a density at $x=0$ gives another density, since
densities enter only through integrals. The former core theorem that assumed it was therefore vacuous. It
is replaced by the almost-everywhere form \lean{AEEnstrophyWeight} (on regular $\omega$).
\item \textbf{Almost-everywhere weight hypothesis over all densities}
(\lean{AEEnstrophyWeightUnrestricted}: every density, measurable or not, equals $-c\,\mathcal E(\omega)$ a.e.\ at
regular $\omega$). \emph{Refuted} for $T$: \lean{TetherWeight.not_aeEnstrophyWeightUnrestricted_tetherKernel}.
Let $V\subset B(0,1)$ contain one point of each class of $\R^3/\mathbb Q e_0$ meeting the unit ball
(\lean{vitaliSet}). Its disjoint rational translates show that every null-measurable subset of $V$ is null,
and they cover $B(0,1)$, so $V$ is not null. Hence $\int 1_V f=0$ for every $f$
(\lean{integral_indicator_vitaliSet_mul}): either $1_V f$ is not integrable, or its support is a
null-measurable subset of $V$. At the regular vorticity $\omega=0$ we have $\mathcal E(0)=0$, so $T(0;\cdot,\cdot)=0$ and
$1_V$ is a density of $T$ there, but $1_V\neq0$ on a non-null set. Every kernel equal to $T$ therefore fails this
hypothesis (\lean{not_aeEnstrophyWeightUnrestricted_of_eq_tether}), so the core theorem stated with it had jointly
unsatisfiable hypotheses. \lean{AEEnstrophyWeight} now quantifies only over densities that are locally
integrable at every regular $\omega$, and \texttt{h\_density} asks for such a density; $T$ has one
(\lean{tetherKernel_has_locallyIntegrable_density}).
\item \textbf{\lean{Step1Hyp}} (derivative of a difference is the difference of derivatives, for all
functionals). \emph{Refuted}: \lean{not_step1Hyp}, via \lean{TetherNondegeneracy.not_forall_functionalDerivative_sub}.
Deleted with it: \lean{step1_locality_sym}, \lean{step1_locality_route1}, \lean{tetherKernel_polarizes}.
\item \textbf{Global equations in the Kato witness.} The field \lean{pde : NS_PDE u p ν} of
\lean{KatoLocalWitness} asked for the equations at every $t\ge0$, which is stronger than Kato's local theorem.
It is now \lean{pde : NS_PDE_On u p ν T} (equations on $[0,T]$) with \lean{phys : IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn u T}.
Deleted because they used the global field: the restart concatenation
\lean{mem_existenceTimes_add_of_kato_restart} (which would need a gluing statement),
\lean{conditional_regularity_of_bkmIntegrability}, \lean{conditional_regularity_scaffold},
\lean{conditional_regularity_of_restart}, \lean{conditional_regularity_of_bkm_uniqueness} and
\lean{conditional_regularity_of_kato_estimates}. The hypotheses \texttt{hcrit} (BKM criterion via
\lean{Tstar}) and the global-path rate in \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem} are replaced by the explicit
continuation hypothesis \lean{BKMGlobalContinuation} and the local-solution rate \lean{LocalTetherRate}
(Section~\ref{sec:analytic}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The encoding at non-regular vorticities.} At a vorticity with a non-differentiable component,
\lean{FunctionalDerivative F ω} is $0$ for every $F$
(\lean{functionalDerivative_indep_of_not_differentiable}), so
$T(\omega;F,G)=0$ there (\lean{tetherKernel_indep_of_not_differentiable}). This is now a property of a genuine
derivative (no derivative exists there), not an artefact of an unspecified choice. The record lemma
\lean{polarizes_tetherKernel_zero_of_not_differentiable} remains true but its hypothesis is refuted.

\section{The obstruction to damping}\label{sec:damping}

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{Combined.left_H_zero_of_C2_symm}]\label{thm:obstr}
Let $B$ be any kernel that is symmetric, $B(\omega;F,G)=B(\omega;G,F)$, and satisfies (C2). Then
$B(\omega;H,M)=0$ for every $\omega$ and every functional $M$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[\leanbrk{Combined.tetherKernel_left_H_zero}]\label{cor:obstr}
If $T$ satisfies (C2), then $T(\omega;H,M)=T(\omega;M,H)=0$ for every $\omega$ and $M$.
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm:obstr} is unconditional. For the tether, Corollary~\ref{cor:obstr} requires (C2) as a
hypothesis, and (C2) for $T$ is not proved (Section~\ref{sec:ke}). The interpretation, for $T$ or any
symmetric kernel that satisfies (C2), is the following. Along the tethered flow
$\dot M=\{M,H\}_{\Tf}=\{M,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}+T(M,H)$, the tether term would vanish; hence the symmetric design that
would make the tether preserve the Euler vector field (C2) would also force its contribution to the evolution of every
functional, including smoothed versions of $\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$, to be zero. The negative diagonal values
$T(F,F)\le0$ of (C3) never enter dynamics generated by $H$. Consequently the cubic rate
$M'\le CM^2-\kappa''M^3$ used below is not a consequence of the tether: under (C2) its damping coefficient
would be $0$, and without (C2) nothing about it is proved.

A related formal fact: \lean{PlanSteps.tether_bound_not_scale_invariant}: for
$\kappa>0$, $M\neq0$ and any real $S$, the family of inequalities
$\lambda^6S\le C(\lambda^2M)^3-\kappa(\lambda^2M)^4$ ($\lambda>0$) fails for some $\lambda$, so a pointwise bound of
this shape cannot be invariant under Navier--Stokes scaling.

\section{Canonicity}\label{sec:canon}

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether}; delegates to \leanbrk{uniqueness_of_minimal_symmetric_tether}]\label{thm:canon}
Let a group act on \lean{CoadjointOrbit} and assume \lean{SDiffActionHyp} (proved for the concrete group). Let $B$ be symmetric, satisfy
(C1), (C2), (C3), \lean{SaturatesTetherQuadratic B} (i.e.\ $B(\omega;F,F)=T(\omega;F,F)$ for all $F,\omega$) and
\lean{PolarizesOnDifferentiable B} (the polarization identity
$2B(F,G)=B(F{+}G,F{+}G)-B(F,F)-B(G,G)$ whenever $F$ and $G$ have functional gradients at $\omega$). Then
$B(\omega;F,G)=T(\omega;F,G)$ whenever $F$ and $G$ have functional gradients at $\omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The proof uses only saturation, polarization of $B$, and polarization of $T$ on functionals with gradients,
which is now \emph{proved} (\lean{tetherKernel_polarizesOnDifferentiable}). The hypotheses \lean{SDiffActionHyp}
and (C1)--(C3) are not used. Theorem~\ref{thm:canon} is therefore a uniqueness statement \emph{given} agreement
on the diagonal, not a derivation of $T$ from (C1)--(C3). Unrestricted polarization of $T$ is false
(Section~\ref{sec:removed}).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether_core}]\label{thm:canoncore}
Let $B$ be symmetric and assume: $B(\omega;F,G)$ depends on $F$ only through $\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega$
(\texttt{h\_proj}); if $B$ factors through the projection then it has a density $\alpha$
(\lean{HasTetherKernelDensity}) with $\alpha(\omega,\cdot)$ locally integrable at every regular $\omega$
(\texttt{h\_density}); every such locally integrable density satisfies, for some $c$ and every
regular $\omega$, $\alpha(\omega,x)=-c\,\mathcal E(\omega)$ for almost every $x$
(\texttt{h\_weight : AEEnstrophyWeight B}); and $B$ saturates $T$ on the diagonal (\texttt{h\_sat}). Then $B=T$.
\end{theorem}

$F_\varphi(\omega)=\int\langle\omega,\varphi\rangle$. The witness uses rotation fields
$\rho_c(y)=\chi(1-|y-c|^2)\,R(y-c)$ ($\chi$ a smooth cut-off, $R$ a rotation generator) with disjoint supports at

\section{Concentrated divergence-free test fields and the weight condition}\label{sec:testfields}

\paragraph{The family.} For a smooth compactly supported scalar $H$ put
$\mathrm{curl}(He_2)=(\partial_1H,-\partial_0H,0)$ (\lean{curlVal}, with $\partial_iH(y)$ Mathlib's
\lean{fderiv ℝ H y (EuclideanSpace.single i 1)}). It is smooth, has compact support inside
\lean{tsupport H}, and is divergence-free (\lean{div_curlVal}, by symmetry of second derivatives,
Mathlib \lean{ContDiffAt.isSymmSndFDerivAt}); hence it is a test direction (\lean{curlOrbit_test}). With $H$ a
Mathlib \lean{ContDiffBump} centred at $x$ ($1$ on $B(x,r/4)$, $0$ outside $B(x,r/2)$),
\lean{concField x hr} is a test field supported in $B(x,r)$ (\lean{tsupport_concField_subset}) and not
identically zero (\lean{concField_ne_zero}), for every point $x$ and every radius $r>0$.

\paragraph{Orthogonality to $u$.} The tether uses $\Pi_uv=v-\lambda(v)u$ with
$\lambda(v)=\int\langle v,u\rangle/\int\|u\|^2$ (\lean{projCoef}), and $u=\BS\,\omega$ is not compactly supported,
so the concentrated fields are not themselves orthogonal to $u$. The proof avoids constructing orthogonal
fields: $\lambda$ is additive on test fields (\lean{projCoef_add_smul}), and two auxiliary test fields with
$\lambda\neq0$, supported away from the support of $a,b$, can be subtracted from $a$ and $b$ to make them
orthogonal to $u$ without changing $\langle a,b\rangle$ pointwise (\lean{good_of_two_correctors}). This works
near every point except at most two (\lean{bad_finite}), a null set.

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{tetherKernel_aeEnstrophyWeight}]\label{thm:weight}
\lean{AEEnstrophyWeight TetherKernel} holds with $c=\kappa$: every density $\alpha$ of $T$ with $\alpha(\omega,\cdot)$
locally integrable at every regular $\omega$ satisfies $\alpha(\omega,x)=-\kappa\,\mathcal E(\omega)$ for almost every
$x$, at every regular $\omega$.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Proof (formalized).} Put $\beta=\alpha(\omega,\cdot)+\kappa\mathcal E(\omega)$ (locally integrable). For test fields
$a,b$ with $\lambda(a)=\lambda(b)=0$, the gradients of the linear functionals $F_a,F_b$ are $a,b$, $\Pi_ua=a$,
$\Pi_ub=b$ a.e., and the density equation gives $\int\beta\langle a,b\rangle=0$
(\lean{integral_beta_eq_zero}); all products are integrable (Mathlib
\texttt{LocallyIntegrable.}\allowbreak\texttt{integrable\_smul\_right\_of\_}\allowbreak\texttt{hasCompactSupport}). With the correctors this holds for all test
pairs supported in a small ball around any good point. On such a ball, for every smooth $g$ with compact
support in it and a bump $\chi\equiv1$ near $\operatorname{supp}g$,
$g=\langle\mathrm{curl}(x_0g\,e_2),\mathrm{curl}(x_0\chi\,e_2)\rangle-\langle\mathrm{curl}(g\,e_2),\mathrm{curl}(\tfrac12x_0^2\chi\,e_2)\rangle$
pointwise (\lean{pointwise_identity}), so $\int\beta g=0$, and Mathlib's
\texttt{IsOpen.}\allowbreak\texttt{ae\_eq\_zero\_of\_integral\_}\allowbreak\texttt{contDiff\_smul\_eq\_zero} gives $\beta=0$ a.e.\ on the ball
(\lean{ae_zero_on_good_ball}). A countable subcover (Mathlib \lean{TopologicalSpace.isOpen_iUnion_countable})
and the finite bad set give $\beta=0$ a.e. No Lebesgue-differentiation step is needed beyond Mathlib's
fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations.

\paragraph{Sanity.} The hypotheses are satisfiable and the conclusion is not vacuous
(\lean{aeEnstrophyWeight_nontrivial}): the canonical density is a locally integrable density of $T$, and at the
regular vorticity $\rho_0$ the weight $-\kappa\mathcal E(\rho_0)$ is nonzero. The unrestricted form over all densities
is false (Section~\ref{sec:removed}). Consequently the density and weight inputs of
\lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether_core} hold for $B=T$ (\lean{tetherKernel_core_inputs}); for a general
competitor $B$ they remain hypotheses.

\section{The analytic layer}\label{sec:analytic}

\begin{lemma}[Comparison; \leanbrk{AnalyticPipeline.comparison_ode_stability}]\label{lem:cmp}
Let $C,\kappa''>0$ and let $y:\R\to\R$ be differentiable at every $s\ge0$ with
$y'(s)\le Cy(s)^2-\kappa''y(s)^3$ for $s\ge0$. Then $y(t)\le\max(y(0),C/\kappa'')$ for every $t\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Finite-interval comparison; \leanbrk{comparison_ode_stability_Icc}]
The same conclusion holds on $[0,T]$: if $y$ is differentiable on $[0,T]$ with $y'\le Cy^2-\kappa''y^3$ there,
then $y(t)\le\max(y(0),C/\kappa'')$ for $t\in[0,T]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[One-sided comparison, new in 0.1.5]\label{lem:cmpright}
\emph{Lean:} \leanbrk{TetheredLyapunov.comparison_ode_stability_Icc_right}.\par\noindent
Let $C,\kappa''>0$ and let $y$ be continuous on $[0,T]$ with, at every $s\in[0,T)$, a right derivative
$d$ (\lean{HasDerivWithinAt y d (Set.Ici s) s}) satisfying $d\le Cy(s)^2-\kappa''y(s)^3$. Then
$y(t)\le\max(y(0),C/\kappa'')$ for $t\in[0,T]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[\leanbrk{riccati_ceiling_of_vorticity_di}]
If $t\mapsto M(t)=\sup_x\|(\nabla\times u(t))(x)\|$ is continuous, differentiable at every $s\ge0$, and
satisfies $M'(s)\le CM(s)^2-\kappa''M(s)^3$ for $s\ge0$ with $C,\kappa''>0$, then $M$ is bounded on $[0,\infty)$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Local solutions.} \lean{NS_PDE_On u p ν T} asks for the equations for $t\in[0,T]$ only, and
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn u T} is the physical-solution predicate on $[0,T]$.
\lean{IsLocalPhysicalSolution ν T u₀ u p} combines $u(0)=u_0$, \lean{NS_PDE_On}, smoothness on $[0,T]$ and
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn}.

The following hypotheses are named propositions (exact text in Appendix~\ref{app:lean:analytic}); all are
open for general data or are classical theorems that are not formalized:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \lean{KatoLocalWitness u₀ ν}: a time $T>0$ and $(u,p)$ with $u(0)=u_0$, smooth on $[0,T]$, satisfying
\lean{NS_PDE_On u p ν T} and \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn u T}. This is the conclusion of Kato's local theorem
\cite{Kato1984} for the library's data class; it is not formalized, and a witness is an input (hKato).
\item \lean{LocalTetherRate u₀ ν} (hDI; corrected in 0.1.5, Section~\ref{sec:corr}): there are $C,\kappa''>0$,
fixed before the interval, such that every local physical solution from $u_0$ on every $[0,T]$ has $M$
continuous on $[0,T]$, a right derivative $d\le CM(s)^2-\kappa''M(s)^3$ at every $s\in[0,T)$, and $\omega(t)$
bounded for $t\in[0,T]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:cmpright} it implies \lean{LocalTetherBound u₀ ν}
(\lean{localTetherBound_of_localTetherRate}); it holds at $u_0=0$ (\lean{localTetherRate_zero}).
\item \lean{LocalTetherBound u₀ ν} (corrected in 0.1.5): one ceiling $Y$ such that, for every local physical
solution from $u_0$ on every $[0,T]$ and every $t\in[0,T]$, $\omega(t)$ is bounded and $M(t)\le Y$.
\lean{ClayStatements.LocalTetherClosingBound ν} asserts this for all smooth divergence-free finite-energy data
with bounded initial vorticity. It implies \lean{LocalFiniteTimeVorticityBound u₀ ν} (for each $T_0$ one
ceiling, with the same boundedness clause, on intervals $[0,T]$, $T\le T_0$).
\item \lean{BKMGlobalContinuation u₀ ν} (hcont): if a Kato witness exists and
\lean{LocalFiniteTimeVorticityBound u₀ ν} holds, then there are $u,p$ with $u(0)=u_0$, \lean{NS_PDE u p ν},
$u(t)$ smooth for $t\ge0$ and \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution u}. This packages Kato local existence with the
Beale--Kato--Majda continuation criterion \cite{BKM1984}; it is a classical-theorem input. \emph{Caveat:} the
classical statements are for $H^s$ data, while the library's data class is $C^\infty\cap L^2$ on $\R^3$.
\item \lean{SmoothPhysicalUniqueness u₀ ν} (huniq): two local physical solutions from $u_0$ agree on the common
interval; a classical-theorem input, used only for uniqueness clauses.
\end{itemize}
The global-path predicates \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate}, \lean{FrohmanianTetherBound} and
\lean{FrohmanianTetherClosingBound} are kept for auxiliary lemmas; the auxiliary predicates
\lean{UniformVorticityCeiling}, \lean{FiniteTimeVorticityBound}, \lean{BKMIntegrability},
\lean{BKMContinuationCriterionFor}, \lean{SmoothBelowTstarFor} and \lean{ClosureBlueprint.RiccatiMajorantExists}
now quantify only over physical solutions.

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{TetheredLyapunov.conditional_regularity}; also \leanbrk{ClosureBlueprint.conditional_regularity_of_open_hyps}]\label{thm:blue}
Let $w$ be a \lean{KatoLocalWitness u₀ ν} and assume \lean{BKMGlobalContinuation u₀ ν} and
\lean{LocalTetherBound u₀ ν}. Then there exist $u,p$ with \lean{NS_PDE u p ν}, $u(0)=u_0$,
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution u}, and $u(t)$ smooth and divergence-free for every $t\ge0$ (and $M(t)\ge0$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{ConditionalRegularity.conditional_regularity_of_open_hyps}]\label{thm:cr}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:blue} for $u_0(0)$ (stated for a time-dependent field $u_0(\cdot)$ that is
divergence-free at all times, with $u_0(0)$ smooth and of finite energy), together with
\lean{SmoothPhysicalUniqueness (u0 0) ν}, there is a $u$ with $u(0)=u_0(0)$,
$u(t)$ smooth and satisfying \lean{satisfies_NavierStokes} (physical solution of the equations) for $t\ge0$,
and unique among smooth fields with these properties.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\leanbrk{frohmanian_tether_theorem}]\label{thm:fft}
Assume: (hKato) a Kato local witness for every smooth divergence-free finite-energy $u_0$ and $\nu>0$;
(hcont) \lean{BKMGlobalContinuation u₀ ν} for all $u_0$ and all $\nu>0$; and (hDI) \lean{LocalTetherRate u₀ ν}
for all $\nu>0$ and all $u_0$ with bounded vorticity. Then there is a kernel $\Tf_F$ such that: (1) every kernel
with density equal to the canonical tether density equals $\Tf_F$; and (2) for every $\nu>0$ and every smooth
divergence-free finite-energy $u_0$ with bounded vorticity there are $u,p$ with \lean{NS_PDE u p ν}, $u(0)=u_0$,
and $u(t)$ smooth and divergence-free for all $t\ge0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Clause (2) is a global-existence statement \emph{conditional on} (hDI) for all data, which is as strong as
the open problem, together with the classical inputs (hKato) and (hcont); it is not a regularity
theorem. The proof goes through the finite-interval comparison lemma (a time-uniform ceiling on all local
solutions), then the continuation hypothesis. In version 0.1.4 the hypothesis (hDI) was unsatisfiable
(Theorem~\ref{thm:escape}), so the 0.1.4 statement was vacuous; the 0.1.5 statement is not.
Version 0.1.1 had a clause assuming $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$; draft versions also had an antisymmetric
clause, the hypotheses \texttt{hTlb}, \texttt{hInt}, \texttt{huniq\_shift}, and then \texttt{hcrit},
\texttt{huniq} with a globally defined Kato witness. All were removed (Section~\ref{sec:removed}).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Non-vacuity at zero data; \leanbrk{conditional_regularity_nonvacuous_at_zero}]
For every $\nu>0$ there is $u$ with $u(0)=0$, smooth and a physical solution for $t\ge0$, unique among smooth
physical solutions with zero data. It is proved from \lean{kato_witness_zero} and the zero-data instances
\lean{smoothPhysicalUniqueness_zero}, \lean{localTetherBound_zero}, \lean{bkmGlobalContinuation_zero}. Also
\lean{stepField_not_physical}: a time-discontinuous step field, which
would satisfy \lean{NS_PDE} because \lean{deriv} is $0$ at non-differentiable points, is excluded by
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution}.
\end{theorem}

This shows only that the conclusion form of Theorem~\ref{thm:cr} is consistent in the trivial case; it gives
no information on nonzero data. Section~\ref{sec:explicit} shows why the explicit periodic flows cannot replace
the zero instance: for nonzero periodic data no \lean{KatoLocalWitness} exists.

\section{The periodic Clay statement and a conditional reduction}\label{sec:clay}

\lean{ClayStatements.Clay_B_exact} formalizes a version of Fefferman's statement (B) \cite{Fefferman2006}:
for every $\nu>0$ and every smooth, divergence-free, $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic $u_0$ on $\R^3$ there is a
solution $(u,p)$ that is $C^\infty$ on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, solves the equations with zero forcing, has
$u(0)=u_0$, and is periodic for all $t\ge0$. (Fefferman's formulation also contains a bounded-energy
condition; the formal statement is as quoted in Appendix~\ref{app:lean:clay}.)

The Main Theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:mainA}, \lean{clay_B_exact_of_periodic_tether_bound}) concludes
\lean{Clay_B_exact} from \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} and \lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation ν} for every
$\nu>0$. Its two hypotheses are open, and together they are at least as strong as the conclusion; the theorem
isolates the tether bound as the single estimate that, with classical continuation, yields (B).

\section{Explicit exact solutions: decaying shear and ABC flows}\label{sec:explicit}

Coordinates are $x=(x_0,x_1,x_2)$ (Lean indices \lean{0}, \lean{1}, \lean{2}). The module \lean{ExplicitFlows}
checks two classical moving solutions against the library's own equations \lean{navier_stokes_eq}
$\partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u$, $\operatorname{div}u=0$ (for all $t\ge0$), hence
\lean{NS_PDE} and \lean{NS_PDE_On} for every $T$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \emph{decaying shear} $u(t,x)=e^{-\nu k^2t}\,(a\sin(kx_2),0,0)$ with constant pressure $p=0$
(\lean{shearFlow}, \lean{shearFlow_apply}, \lean{shearFlow_navier_stokes}, \lean{shearFlow_NS_PDE_On});
\item \emph{decaying ABC (Arnold--Beltrami--Childress) flow}
$u(t,x)=e^{-\nu k^2t}\,(A\sin kx_2+C\cos kx_1,\ B\sin kx_0+A\cos kx_2,\ C\sin kx_1+B\cos kx_0)$ with pressure
$p=-|u|^2/2$ (\lean{abcFlow}, \lean{abcProfile_apply}, \lean{abcPressure}, \lean{abcPressure₀_eq_norm},
\lean{abcFlow_navier_stokes}, \lean{abcFlow_NS_PDE_On}). Its profile is a curl eigenfield,
$\nabla\times u=k\,u$ (\lean{curl_abcProfile}).
\end{itemize}
Both statements hold for all real $\nu,k,a,A,B,C$. Both come from one mechanism
(\lean{flow_navier_stokes}): a trigonometric profile $V$ (\lean{trigField}) has $\Delta V=-k^2V$
(\lean{laplacian_trigField}); if $\operatorname{div}V=0$ and $(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla p_0=0$, then
$u=\varphi(t)V$, $p=\varphi(t)^2p_0$ with $\varphi'=-\nu k^2\varphi$ solves the equations. For the shear profile
$(V\cdot\nabla)V=0$; for the ABC profile $(V\cdot\nabla)V=\nabla(|V|^2/2)$ and the sign of $p_0=-|V|^2/2$ matches the
library's convention $+\nabla p$ on the left (\lean{abc_balance}). The time factor \lean{decay} is $e^{-\nu k^2t}$
for $t\ge0$ and is continued affinely for $t<0$, because \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn} requires every solution to be
affine for $t\le0$; the two-sided time derivative at $t=0$ is then $-\nu k^2u(0)$ (\lean{hasDerivAt_decay}).

\paragraph{Genuine and nonzero.} For $t\ge0$, $a\neq0$ and $k\neq0$ the shear flow is not identically zero
(\lean{shearFlow_ne_zero}); the ABC flow at the origin equals $e^{-\nu k^2t}(C,A,B)$
(\lean{abcFlow_apply_zero}) and is nonzero unless $A=B=C=0$ (\lean{abcFlow_ne_zero}). Each time slice is
$C^\infty$ (\lean{flow_smooth}), and the time-differentiability, continuity and affine-extension clauses of
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn} hold (\lean{flow_physical_clauses}).

\paragraph{Periodic class: genuine instances.} With $k=2\pi m$, $m\in\mathbb Z$, both flows are $\mathbb Z^3$-periodic
and jointly $C^\infty$ on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, with one-sided time derivative at $t=0$, i.e.\ global smooth
periodic solutions in the class \lean{IsPeriodicSmoothSolution} of \lean{Clay_B_exact} with zero forcing
(\lean{shearFlow_isPeriodicSmoothSolution}, \lean{abcFlow_isPeriodicSmoothSolution}). So their initial data
satisfy all hypotheses of \lean{Clay_B_exact} and its conclusion holds \emph{for these data}
(\lean{clay_B_instance_shear}, \lean{clay_B_instance_abc}); the conclusion of \lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation}
therefore holds at these data without that hypothesis, and the binders of \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound} and
\lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation} are non-vacuous there (\lean{shearFlow_isPeriodicSmoothSolutionOn},
\lean{abcFlow_isPeriodicSmoothSolutionOn}). This is a
statement about two explicit families of data and is not a step toward \lean{Clay_B_exact}; no Clay Millennium
claim is made. The periodic closing bound \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound} is \emph{not} discharged at these data:
it quantifies over every local smooth periodic solution from $u_0$, and uniqueness of such solutions is not
formalized.

\paragraph{They are not Kato witnesses; no witness exists for periodic data.} A \lean{KatoLocalWitness} carries
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolutionOn}, which requires $\int_{\R^3}\|u(t)\|^2<\infty$. The library proves that no continuous,
$\mathbb Z^3$-periodic, not identically zero field has $\|u\|^2$ integrable on $\R^3$
(\leanl{not_integrable_sq_of_periodic}: the set $\{\|u\|^2\ge c\}$ contains infinitely many disjoint translates of a
ball), hence $\mathrm{KatoLocalWitness}\ u_0\ \nu$ is empty for every such $u_0$
(\leanl{isEmpty_katoLocalWitness_of_periodic}), in particular for the shear and ABC data
(\leanl{isEmpty_katoLocalWitness_shear}, \leanl{isEmpty_katoLocalWitness_abc}). The planned witness
\texttt{kato\_witness\_shear} therefore cannot exist, and the $\R^3$ chain of Section~\ref{sec:analytic} cannot be
instantiated on these flows. Moreover, at such data the $\R^3$-class hypotheses \lean{LocalTetherBound},
\lean{SmoothPhysicalUniqueness} and \lean{BKMGlobalContinuation} hold only \emph{vacuously}
(\leanl{localTetherBound_vacuous_of_periodic}, \leanl{smoothPhysicalUniqueness_vacuous_of_periodic},
\leanl{bkmGlobalContinuation_vacuous_of_periodic}): there is no local physical solution to quantify over, and the
premise $\mathrm{Nonempty}(\mathrm{KatoLocalWitness}\ u_0\ \nu)$ is false. These are recorded as an audit of the
encoding and are \emph{not} counted as discharged hypotheses. A nonzero finite-energy exact solution on $\R^3$ (and
hence a nonzero \lean{KatoLocalWitness}) is not constructed. (The library already contained the constant flows
$u\equiv c$, \leanl{nonzero_smooth_ns_solution_exists}, which are stationary and also have infinite energy.)

\section{What the Lean development does and does not prove}\label{sec:does}

\paragraph{Proved (no further hypotheses).}
Symmetry of $T$ and (C3); antisymmetry of $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $\{H,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}=0$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstr} ($B(H,M)=0$ for every symmetric $B$ satisfying (C2)); the refutation
Theorem~\ref{thm:keref} of $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$ and the non-uniqueness Theorem~\ref{thm:nouniq};
\lean{SDiffActionHyp} for
the concrete permutation group; the refutations of Section~\ref{sec:removed}; uniqueness of functional
gradients; nondegeneracy, polarization on differentiable functionals, saturation, projection-dependence and
the canonical density for $T$; the corrected weight condition \lean{AEEnstrophyWeight TetherKernel}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:weight}); the transported (C1) for $T$ under translations (Theorem~\ref{thm:c1t}); the finite-interval comparison lemma; the sanity lemma for \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution}; the comparison Lemma~\ref{lem:cmp}; the scaling
identity and the scale non-invariance lemma; the zero-data facts and \lean{stepField_not_physical}; the Fourier-symbol identities
\lean{PlanSteps.bsSymbol_div}, \lean{PlanSteps.bsSymbol_curl} (mode-wise algebra, not connected to the
real-space Biot--Savart integral); the explicit shear and ABC solutions, their non-triviality and periodic
smoothness, and the non-existence of Kato witnesses for nonzero periodic data (Section~\ref{sec:explicit});
the one-sided comparison Lemma~\ref{lem:cmpright}; the audit results of Section~\ref{sec:corr} (junk value of the
supremum, the degenerate local solution, the refutation of the 0.1.4 \lean{LocalTetherRate}, and
\lean{localTetherRate_zero}).

\paragraph{Proved as implications from named, open hypotheses.}
The Main Theorem~\ref{thm:mainA} (from the tether bound and periodic BKM continuation) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:mainB} (BKM integrability from the tether bound); canonicity (from saturation and polarization); Casimir conservation (from \lean{IsCoadjointMotion}); the
conditional regularity theorems \ref{thm:blue}, \ref{thm:cr}, \ref{thm:fft} (from rate or bound
hypotheses on local solutions, Kato existence, Kato--BKM continuation and uniqueness). The hypotheses are
those of Section~\ref{sec:conditional}, annotated in Section~\ref{sec:assumptions}.

\paragraph{Not proved, and not claimed.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Global existence, smoothness or uniqueness for 3D Navier--Stokes with large data; any part of the Clay
Millennium Problem.
\item The Frohmanian tether bound \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound} or \lean{PeriodicTetherBound} at any nonzero datum;
the converse BKM integrability $\Rightarrow$ tether bound and its pillars (P1)--(P3) (Section~\ref{sec:converse}).
\item The cubic rate \lean{LocalTetherRate} (or \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate}), any closing bound, or any damping mechanism from the tether
(under (C2) its damping coefficient would be $0$).
\item (C2) for $T$. The identification $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$ is refuted (Theorem~\ref{thm:keref}); the
classical identification $\delta H/\delta\omega=\psi$ (stream function) is not formalized.
\item The transported form of (C1) for $T$ under rotations or general volume-preserving diffeomorphisms
(only translations are proved, Theorem~\ref{thm:c1t}; the fixed-functional form is refuted,
Theorem~\ref{thm:c1ref}). That the Euler flow is a coadjoint motion.
\item A Jacobi identity for $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Tf}$ (the former conditional lemma was circular and is deleted), or a Poisson structure; the Jacobi identity for
$\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ (cited only).
\item Kato local existence \cite{Kato1984}, Beale--Kato--Majda \cite{BKM1984}, gluing of local solutions,
parabolic smoothing, energy uniqueness: all are inputs, none is formalized.
\item Anything on the quotient torus: \lean{T3} is $\R^3$, and no diffeomorphism group of $\T$ is constructed.
\item Derivation of $T$ from (C1)--(C3): canonicity assumes diagonal agreement with $T$.
\item Any nonzero Navier--Stokes solution of finite energy on $\R^3$, and any nonzero \lean{KatoLocalWitness}; the
sanity path for \lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution} is not a solution. (Nonzero solutions of infinite energy are proved:
the constant flows and the shear and ABC flows of Section~\ref{sec:explicit}.)
\item Uniqueness of smooth periodic solutions, hence \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound} even at the explicit data.
\end{itemize}

The physical motivations of earlier versions (fluid--gravity duality, Onsager's conjecture
\cite{Isett2018}, metriplectic viscous completion) are not formalized and play no role in any statement here.

\paragraph{Reproduction (one command).} From a fresh clone:
\begin{Verbatim}
git clone https://github.com/BenFrohman/frohmanian-tether-sdiff
cd frohmanian-tether-sdiff
bash scripts/verify.sh        # equivalently: make verify
\end{Verbatim}
\texttt{scripts/verify.sh} installs \texttt{elan} if needed (which installs the toolchain pinned in
\texttt{lean-toolchain}), runs \texttt{lake exe cache get} (Mathlib cache for the revision pinned in
\texttt{lake-manifest.json}), \texttt{lake build}, \texttt{python3 scripts/check\_no\_sorry.py}, the strict audit
\texttt{lake env lean scripts/AxiomAudit.lean}, and \texttt{lake env lean scripts/PrintAxioms.lean}
(\texttt{\#print axioms} for the headline theorems); it exits non-zero on any failure and ends with
\texttt{VERIFY OK}. The \emph{Lean certificate} CI workflow runs the same script.
\texttt{scripts/AxiomAudit.lean} computes \texttt{collectAxioms} for every non-internal declaration in the
\texttt{FrohmanianTether} modules and raises an error if any axiom other than the three standard ones occurs
(this includes \texttt{sorryAx}, produced by \texttt{sorry}, and \texttt{Lean.ofReduceBool}, produced by
\texttt{native\_decide}) or if any axiom is declared. \texttt{scripts/check\_no\_sorry.py} rejects the tokens
\texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit}, \texttt{native\_decide} and \texttt{axiom} declarations outside comments and
strings.
