\begin{abstract}
We describe a Lean~4/Mathlib formalization of the \emph{Frohmanian tether}, a symmetric quadratic
correction $T(F,G)=-\kappa\,\|\omega\|_{L^2}^2\int\langle\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega,\Pi_u\,\delta G/\delta\omega\rangle$
added to the Arnold Lie--Poisson bracket of ideal fluids, where $\Pi_u$ is the $L^2$-orthogonal projection
away from the velocity $u$ and $\kappa=3/2$. The library proves: the negative-feedback condition (C3)
unconditionally; the kinetic-energy degeneracy (C2) and coadjoint invariance (C1) under explicitly named
hypotheses; a uniqueness (``canonicity'') statement inside a restricted kernel class; and an
\emph{obstruction to damping}: for every symmetric kernel satisfying (C2), $T(M,H)=T(H,M)=0$ for every
functional $M$, so the tether contributes no damping to any functional, in particular not to
$\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$. On the analytic side the library proves a scalar comparison lemma for
$y'\le Cy^2-\kappa''y^3$ and several conditional implications of the form ``(cubic rate or uniform vorticity
bound) $+$ (Kato local existence) $+$ (Beale--Kato--Majda continuation) $+$ (uniqueness) $\Rightarrow$
global smooth solution'', in which every analytic input is an explicit hypothesis. The rate and
closing-bound hypotheses are open, are not supplied by the tether (its damping coefficient is $0$), and are
of the same strength as the global regularity problem. \textbf{This work does not prove global regularity of
the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations and makes no claim toward the Clay Millennium Problem.}
The build (Lean \texttt{v4.30.0-rc1}, Mathlib \texttt{v4.30.0-rc1}, 2712 jobs) contains 519 theorem
constants (483 stated in the sources and 36 auto-generated equation, congruence and injectivity lemmas) and
761 declarations in 22 modules, with no \texttt{sorry}, no \texttt{native\_decide}, no axiom
declarations, and every declaration depending only on \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}; this is enforced in continuous integration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and scope}\label{sec:intro}

Arnold's geometric picture of ideal hydrodynamics \cite{Arnold1966,ArnoldKhesin1998} describes the
Euler equations as Hamiltonian motion on coadjoint orbits of the group $\mathrm{SDiff}$ of
volume-preserving diffeomorphisms, with Lie--Poisson bracket
$\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\omega)=\int\omega\cdot(\delta F/\delta\omega\times\delta G/\delta\omega)$ and kinetic-energy
Hamiltonian $H(\omega)=\tfrac12\int|\BS\,\omega|^2$. The \emph{Frohmanian tether} programme adds to this
bracket a symmetric, negative-semidefinite quadratic term $T$ and asks whether $T$ can supply a damping
mechanism for the vorticity sup-norm $M(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_{L^\infty}$, which by the Beale--Kato--Majda
criterion \cite{BKM1984} controls continuation of smooth Navier--Stokes solutions.

This paper reports exactly what the accompanying Lean~4 library proves. The Lean statements are the
source of truth: every result below is stated to match a checked Lean declaration, with every hypothesis
made explicit, and the exact Lean text is reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:lean}. The main findings are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item The tether $T$ satisfies (C3) unconditionally, (C2) under the identification $\delta H/\delta\omega=u$
together with $\nabla\cdot u=0$ and $u\in L^2$, and (C1) under two named invariance hypotheses
(Section~\ref{sec:tether}).
\item \emph{Obstruction to damping.} For every symmetric kernel $B$ satisfying (C2), $B(H,M)=0$ for every
functional $M$; for $T$ this gives $T(M,H)=T(H,M)=0$ (Section~\ref{sec:damping}). The tether therefore
cannot produce the cubic damping rate on which the programme relied.
\item \emph{Canonicity} holds only inside a restricted class and follows from saturation and polarization
hypotheses; conditions (C1)--(C3) appear in the statement but are not used (Section~\ref{sec:canon}).
\item The analytic layer consists of proved real-variable lemmas and \emph{conditional} implications whose
open hypotheses (a cubic rate or a uniform vorticity bound for all data, Kato local existence, BKM
continuation, uniqueness) are of Clay strength (Sections~\ref{sec:analytic}--\ref{sec:clay}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Scope statement.} No result in this paper or in the library asserts global existence,
smoothness or uniqueness of solutions of the three-dimensional Navier--Stokes equations. Every theorem whose
conclusion mentions global solutions is an implication from hypotheses that are open. Section~\ref{sec:does}
lists precisely what the Lean development does and does not prove.

\section{The formal setting}\label{sec:setting}

We record the definitions as formalized, including the points at which they differ from the classical
objects.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Space.} \lean{T3} is \lean{EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin 3)}, i.e.\ $\R^3$ with Lebesgue measure,
\emph{not} the quotient torus $\R^3/\mathbb{Z}^3$. Periodicity, where needed (Section~\ref{sec:clay}), is
the predicate \lean{IsZ3Periodic} on functions on $\R^3$. The notation $\mathrm{SDiff}(\T)$ in the title
refers to the motivating geometry; no diffeomorphism group of $\T$ is constructed.
\item \textbf{Phase space.} \lean{CoadjointOrbit} is the type of pointwise divergence-free vector fields
$\omega:\R^3\to\R^3$ (a subtype; no orbit structure is built in).
\item \textbf{Functional derivative.} \lean{FunctionalDerivative F ω} is a \emph{choice} of a Gâteaux
representative $\delta$ of $F$ at $\omega$ along $C^1$ divergence-free variations, and is $0$ if none exists.
A Gâteaux representative is in general determined only up to the pairing against admissible variations,
so the chosen field is not canonical.
\item \textbf{Velocity and energy.} \lean{velocity_from_vorticity ω} is the Biot--Savart integral
\lean{BiotSavart ω.val}, and \lean{KineticEnergyHamiltonian ω} $=\tfrac12\int\|\BS\,\omega\|^2$.
\item \textbf{Navier--Stokes.} \lean{navier_stokes_eq u p ν} (and \lean{NS_PDE}) asserts the momentum
equation with Mathlib's total derivative \lean{deriv} in time, and $\nabla\cdot u=0$, pointwise for $t\ge0$.
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution u} adds pointwise time-differentiability for $t\ge0$, spatial continuity,
finite kinetic energy, the energy inequality $\int\|u(t)\|^2\le\int\|u(0)\|^2$, and $u(t)=u(0)$ for $t\le0$.
\end{itemize}
The exact definitions are quoted in Appendix~\ref{app:lean:setting}.

\section{The tether kernel and conditions (C1)--(C3)}\label{sec:tether}

\begin{definition}[\lean{Pi_u}, \lean{TetherKernel}, \lean{TetheredBracket}]
For velocity fields $u,v$ put
$\Pi_u v=v-\bigl(\int\langle v,u\rangle/\int\|u\|^2\bigr)\,u$ (in Lean, division by $0$ is $0$, so $\Pi_u v=v$
when $\int\|u\|^2=0$). With $\kappa=3/2$ (\lean{kappa_eq_three_halves}),
$\mathcal E(\omega)=\int\|\omega\|^2$ (\lean{Enstrophy}) and $u=\BS\,\omega$,
\[
T(\omega;F,G)=-\kappa\,\mathcal E(\omega)\int\bigl\langle\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega,\;\Pi_u\,\delta G/\delta\omega\bigr\rangle\,dx,
\qquad \{F,G\}_{\Tf}=\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}+T(F,G).
\]
\end{definition}

The three conditions are predicates on a kernel $B(\omega;F,G)$:
(C1) \lean{InvariantUnderCoadjointAction}: $B(g\cdot\omega;F,G)=B(\omega;F,G)$ for all $g$ in a group
\lean{SDiff} acting on \lean{CoadjointOrbit};
(C2) \lean{DegenerateWRTKineticEnergy}: $B(\omega;F,H)=0$ for all $F,\omega$;
(C3) \lean{ProducesControllableNegativeFeedback}:
$B(\omega;F,F)\le-\kappa\,\mathcal E(\omega)\int\|\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega\|^2$.

\begin{theorem}[Symmetry and (C3); \lean{tetherKernel_comm}, \lean{tetherKernel_C3}]
For all $\omega,F,G$: $T(\omega;F,G)=T(\omega;G,F)$, and $T$ satisfies (C3). No hypotheses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[(C2), pointwise form; \lean{tetherKernel_degenerates_on_kinetic_energy}]\label{thm:c2pt}
Let $\omega$ be such that \lean{FunctionalDerivative KineticEnergyHamiltonian ω} $=\BS\,\omega$,
$\nabla\cdot(\BS\,\omega)=0$ pointwise, and $\|\BS\,\omega\|^2$ is integrable. Then $T(\omega;F,H)=0$ for every $F$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[(C2), global form; \lean{Combined.tetherKernel_C2_of_KEDeriv}]\label{thm:c2}
Assume \lean{KineticEnergyDerivIsVelocity} (Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:ke}), and that for every $\omega$,
$\nabla\cdot(\BS\,\omega)=0$ pointwise and $\|\BS\,\omega\|^2$ is integrable. Then $T$ satisfies (C2).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[(C1); \lean{tether_coadjoint_invariance}, \lean{Combined.tetherKernel_C1_of_casimir}]\label{thm:c1}
Let a group \lean{SDiff} act on \lean{CoadjointOrbit}. Assume \lean{SDiffActionHyp SDiff} and
\lean{TetherIntegralPreserved SDiff}. Then $T(g\cdot\omega;F,G)=T(\omega;F,G)$ for all $g,F,G,\omega$.
The same conclusion holds with \lean{TetherIntegralPreserved} replaced by a
\lean{VolumePreservingCoadjointAction} structure (\lean{Combined.tetherKernel_C1_of_volumePreserving}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[On the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:c1}]
\lean{SDiffActionHyp} is a structure with two fields: \lean{casimir_invariant} (every Casimir
$\int f(|\omega|)$ is invariant under the action) and \lean{transitive_on_casimir_levels} (fields with equal
Casimirs lie in one orbit). The proof uses only the first field (to obtain invariance of the enstrophy
weight), but the theorem as stated requires the whole structure. The source documentation records that
the transitivity field is not expected to hold for the genuine $\mathrm{SDiff}$ action, whose orbits are
finer than Casimir level sets. \lean{TetherIntegralPreserved} (invariance of the projected integral) is
open. For the concrete group \lean{ConcreteSDiff.SDiffConcrete} of measure-preserving, measurable,
divergence-preserving permutations of $\R^3$ acting by $g\cdot\omega=\omega\circ g^{-1}$ (scalar
composition, not the geometric push-forward $(Dg\,\omega)\circ g^{-1}$), the field
\lean{casimir_invariant} is proved (\lean{ConcreteSDiff.casimir_invariant}); transitivity remains a
hypothesis (\lean{ConcreteSDiff.sdiffActionHyp_of_transitive}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The identification \texorpdfstring{$\delta H/\delta\omega=u$}{dH/dw = u}}\label{sec:ke}

\begin{hypothesis}[\lean{PlanSteps.KineticEnergyDerivIsVelocity}]\label{hyp:ke}
For every $\omega$, \lean{FunctionalDerivative KineticEnergyHamiltonian ω = velocity_from_vorticity ω}.
\end{hypothesis}

This is a named proposition, not a theorem and not an axiom. Its role in the library is exactly the
following: it is a hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:c2} and of the combined capstone
(Theorem~\ref{thm:capstone}); its pointwise instance is a hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:c2pt}, of
\lean{tethered_reproduces_classical_euler}, and of the first clause of \lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fft}). No theorem in the library proves it, and no theorem refutes it.

We believe it is \emph{false as stated}. The Gâteaux derivative of $\tfrac12\int|\BS\,\omega|^2$ in the
direction $\varepsilon$ is $\int\langle\BS\,\omega,\BS\,\varepsilon\rangle=\int\langle\BS^*\BS\,\omega,\varepsilon\rangle$,
so a natural representative is $\BS^*\BS\,\omega$, which in the periodic setting is (up to gauge) the stream
function $\psi$ with $u=\nabla\times\psi$, not the velocity $u$. Moreover \lean{FunctionalDerivative} selects
one representative by choice, so even an identity that held up to the natural equivalence of
representatives would not by itself give the pointwise equality of fields demanded above. If the hypothesis
is false, then Theorem~\ref{thm:c2} and Theorem~\ref{thm:capstone} are true but vacuous, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:c2pt} gives information only at those $\omega$ (if any) at which its first hypothesis holds. Repairing this would
require changing either the bracket's convention for $\delta H/\delta\omega$ or the projection $\Pi_u$
(e.g.\ projecting away from $\BS^*\BS\,\omega$); we do not do so here.

\section{The obstruction to damping}\label{sec:damping}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Combined.left_H_zero_of_C2_symm}]\label{thm:obstr}
Let $B$ be any kernel that is symmetric, $B(\omega;F,G)=B(\omega;G,F)$, and satisfies (C2). Then
$B(\omega;H,M)=0$ for every $\omega$ and every functional $M$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[\lean{Combined.tetherKernel_left_H_zero}]\label{cor:obstr}
If $T$ satisfies (C2), then $T(\omega;H,M)=T(\omega;M,H)=0$ for every $\omega$ and $M$.
\end{corollary}

Theorem~\ref{thm:obstr} is unconditional. For the tether, Corollary~\ref{cor:obstr} requires (C2) as a
hypothesis, which in turn is available only through Theorem~\ref{thm:c2}, i.e.\ under
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:ke}. The interpretation is the following. Along the tethered flow
$\dot M=\{M,H\}_{\Tf}=\{M,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}+T(M,H)$, the tether term vanishes; hence the symmetric design that
makes the tether preserve the Euler vector field (C2) also forces its contribution to the evolution of every
functional, including smoothed versions of $\|\omega\|_{L^\infty}$, to be zero. The negative diagonal values
$T(F,F)\le0$ of (C3) never enter dynamics generated by $H$. Consequently the cubic rate
$M'\le CM^2-\kappa''M^3$ used below is not a consequence of the tether: its damping coefficient is $0$.

Two related formal facts support this picture. \lean{PlanSteps.tether_bound_not_scale_invariant}: for
$\kappa>0$, $M\neq0$ and any real $S$, the family of inequalities
$\lambda^6S\le C(\lambda^2M)^3-\kappa(\lambda^2M)^4$ ($\lambda>0$) fails for some $\lambda$, so a pointwise bound of
this shape cannot be invariant under Navier--Stokes scaling. \lean{tethered_stretching_of_weight}: the
stretching pairing at a maximum plus the diagonal value $T(F,F)$ is at most $(CM-\kappa M^2)M^2$, but only
under the weight hypothesis $M^4\le\mathcal E(\omega)\int\|\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega\|^2$, for which no source is
known.

\section{Canonicity}\label{sec:canon}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether}; delegates to \lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_symmetric_tether}]\label{thm:canon}
Let a group act on \lean{CoadjointOrbit} and assume \lean{SDiffActionHyp}. Let $B$ be symmetric, satisfy
(C1), (C2), (C3), \lean{SaturatesTetherQuadratic B} (i.e.\ $B(\omega;F,F)=T(\omega;F,F)$ for all $F,\omega$),
\lean{Polarizes B} and \lean{Polarizes TetherKernel} (the polarization identity
$2B(F,G)=B(F{+}G,F{+}G)-B(F,F)-B(G,G)$). Then $B=T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
The proof uses only saturation and the two polarization hypotheses: a kernel that agrees with $T$ on the
diagonal and satisfies polarization equals $T$. The hypotheses \lean{SDiffActionHyp} and (C1)--(C3) are not
used. Theorem~\ref{thm:canon} is therefore a uniqueness statement \emph{given} agreement on the diagonal,
not a derivation of $T$ from (C1)--(C3).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{uniqueness_of_minimal_tether_core}]\label{thm:canoncore}
Let $B$ be symmetric and assume: $B(\omega;F,G)$ depends on $F$ only through $\Pi_u\,\delta F/\delta\omega$
(\texttt{h\_proj}); if $B$ factors through the projection then it has a density $\alpha$
(\lean{HasTetherKernelDensity}) (\texttt{h\_dens}); every such density is of the form
$\alpha(\omega,x)=-c\,\mathcal E(\omega)$ (\texttt{h\_weight}); $B$ saturates $T$ on the diagonal
(\texttt{h\_sat}); and there are $\omega,F$ with
$\mathcal E(\omega)\int\langle\Pi_u\delta F/\delta\omega,\Pi_u\delta F/\delta\omega\rangle\neq0$ (\texttt{h\_nondeg}). Then $B=T$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Bracket structure and Casimirs}\label{sec:bracket}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \lean{classicalBracket_antisymm}: $\{F,G\}_{\mathrm{cl}}=-\{G,F\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$; \lean{classicalBracket_H_H}:
$\{H,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}=0$. The Jacobi identity of $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is classical \cite{Arnold1966} and is
\emph{not} formalized.
\item Since $T$ is symmetric and in general nonzero, $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Tf}$ is \emph{not} antisymmetric; it is
of metriplectic type in the sense of Morrison \cite{Morrison1986} (antisymmetric part plus symmetric
negative-semidefinite part). No Jacobi identity is claimed for it.
\item \lean{tethered_jacobi_identity} is a conditional lemma: if the Jacobiator of $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Tf}$ equals a
cyclic Lie-pairing integral (\texttt{hident}) and that integral vanishes (\texttt{hvanish}), then the Jacobiator
vanishes. Both hypotheses are open; the lemma does not establish a Jacobi identity.
\item \lean{PlanSteps.casimir_conserved}: under \lean{SDiffActionHyp} and \lean{IsCoadjointMotion}
(the trajectory is $\omega(t)=g(t)\cdot\omega(0)$, an open hypothesis), every Casimir is constant along the
trajectory. That the Euler flow is such a motion is not proved.
\item \lean{ScalingIdentities.vorticity_rescale}: for $u_\lambda(x)=\lambda u(\lambda x)$,
$\nabla\times u_\lambda(x)=\lambda^2(\nabla\times u)(\lambda x)$ under differentiability at $\lambda x$.
\end{itemize}

\section{The analytic layer}\label{sec:analytic}

\begin{lemma}[Comparison; \lean{AnalyticPipeline.comparison_ode_stability}]\label{lem:cmp}
Let $C,\kappa''>0$ and let $y:\R\to\R$ be differentiable at every $s\ge0$ with
$y'(s)\le Cy(s)^2-\kappa''y(s)^3$ for $s\ge0$. Then $y(t)\le\max(y(0),C/\kappa'')$ for every $t\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[\lean{riccati_ceiling_of_vorticity_di}]
If $t\mapsto M(t)=\sup_x\|(\nabla\times u(t))(x)\|$ is continuous, differentiable at every $s\ge0$, and
satisfies $M'(s)\le CM(s)^2-\kappa''M(s)^3$ for $s\ge0$ with $C,\kappa''>0$, then $M$ is bounded on $[0,\infty)$.
\end{proposition}

The following hypotheses are named propositions (exact text in Appendix~\ref{app:lean:analytic}); all are
open for general data:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate u₀ ν} (R): for $\nu>0$ and every physical solution from $u_0$, $M$ is
continuous, differentiable on $[0,\infty)$, pointwise bounded in space, and satisfies the cubic inequality
for some $C,\kappa''>0$. (Differentiability of a sup-norm in time is itself a strong requirement.)
\item \lean{FrohmanianTetherBound u₀ ν}: every physical solution from $u_0$ has $\sup_{t\ge0}M(t)<\infty$
(with continuity and spatial boundedness). \lean{ClayStatements.FrohmanianTetherClosingBound ν} asserts
this for \emph{all} smooth divergence-free finite-energy data.
\item \lean{KatoLocalWitness u₀ ν}: data of a local smooth physical solution on $[0,T]$, $T>0$
(local existence \cite{Kato1984} is not formalized; a witness is an input).
\item \lean{PhysicalBKMContinuationCriterionFor u₀ ν}: the Beale--Kato--Majda criterion \cite{BKM1984}
for physical solutions from $u_0$, stated via the maximal time \lean{Tstar}; not formalized, an input.
\item \lean{KatoUniquenessOnExistenceTimes u₀ ν w}: every smooth physical solution from $u_0$ agrees
with the witness $w$ on each existence interval; an input.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{ClosureBlueprint.conditional_regularity_of_open_hyps}]\label{thm:blue}
Let $\nu>0$ and $u_0$ smooth, divergence-free, with $\|u_0\|^2$ integrable. Assume
\lean{KatoLocalWitness u₀ ν}, \lean{FrohmanianTetherBound u₀ ν},
\lean{PhysicalBKMContinuationCriterionFor u₀ ν} and \lean{KatoUniquenessOnExistenceTimes u₀ ν w}. Then
there exist $u,p$ with \lean{NS_PDE u p ν}, $u(0)=u_0$, $u(t)$ smooth and divergence-free for every $t\ge0$
(and $M(t)\ge0$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{ConditionalRegularity.conditional_regularity_of_open_hyps}]\label{thm:cr}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:blue} (stated for a time-dependent field $u_0(\cdot)$ that is
divergence-free at all times, with $u_0(0)$ smooth and of finite energy), there is a $u$ with $u(0)=u_0(0)$,
$u(t)$ smooth and satisfying \lean{satisfies_NavierStokes} (physical solution of the equations) for $t\ge0$,
and unique among smooth fields with these properties.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{frohmanian_tether_theorem}]\label{thm:fft}
Assume: (hKato) a Kato local witness for every smooth divergence-free finite-energy datum and $\nu>0$;
(hDI) \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate u₀ ν} for all $u_0,\nu$; a uniform lower bound $\tau>0$ on the witness
times; and the integrability and two uniqueness hypotheses \texttt{hInt}, \texttt{huniq\_shift},
\texttt{huniq} on existence intervals (Appendix~\ref{app:lean:analytic}). Then there is a kernel $\Tf_F$ such
that: (1) for every $F,\omega$ satisfying the three hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:c2pt}, the tethered and
classical brackets agree on $(F,H)$; (2) every \emph{antisymmetric} kernel satisfying (C1)--(C3), saturation
and the polarization hypotheses equals $\Tf_F$; (3) every kernel with density equal to the canonical tether
density equals $\Tf_F$; and (4) for every $\nu>0$ and every smooth divergence-free finite-energy $u_0$ there are
$u,p$ with \lean{NS_PDE u p ν}, $u(0)=u_0$, and $u(t)$ smooth and divergence-free for all $t\ge0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Clause (4) is a global-existence statement \emph{conditional on} (hDI) for all data together with uniform
Kato restart and uniqueness, all of which are open; it is not a regularity theorem. In clause (2),
antisymmetry gives $B(F,F)=0$, so saturation forces $T(\omega;F,F)=0$ for all $F,\omega$, and then
polarization of $T$ forces $T\equiv0$; since $T(\omega;F,F)=-\kappa\,\mathcal E(\omega)\int\|\Pi_u\delta F/\delta\omega\|^2$
is not identically zero in general, clause (2) is expected to apply to no kernel (this observation is ours and
is not formalized).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Non-vacuity at zero data; \lean{conditional_regularity_nonvacuous_at_zero}]
For every $\nu>0$ there is $u$ with $u(0)=0$, smooth and a physical solution for $t\ge0$, unique among smooth
physical solutions with zero data. Also \lean{stepField_not_physical}: a time-discontinuous step field, which
would satisfy \lean{NS_PDE} because \lean{deriv} is $0$ at non-differentiable points, is excluded by
\lean{IsPhysicalNSSolution}.
\end{theorem}

This shows only that the conclusion form of Theorem~\ref{thm:cr} is consistent in the trivial case; it gives
no information on nonzero data.

\section{The combined capstone}\label{sec:capstone}

\begin{theorem}[\lean{Combined.combined_conditional_capstone}]\label{thm:capstone}
Let a group \lean{SDiff} act on \lean{CoadjointOrbit}. Assume all of the following:
\lean{SDiffActionHyp SDiff}; \lean{TetherIntegralPreserved SDiff};
\lean{KineticEnergyDerivIsVelocity}; $\nabla\cdot(\BS\,\omega)=0$ and $\|\BS\,\omega\|^2$ integrable for all $\omega$;
a trajectory $\omega(t)$ with \lean{IsCoadjointMotion}; a symmetric kernel $B$ with \texttt{h\_proj},
\texttt{h\_dens}, \texttt{h\_weight}, \texttt{h\_sat}, \texttt{h\_nondeg} as in Theorem~\ref{thm:canoncore}; $u_0$
smooth, divergence-free, of finite energy; $\nu>0$; \lean{KatoLocalWitness u₀ ν};
\lean{FrohmanianTetherRate u₀ ν}; \lean{FrohmanianTetherClosingBound ν};
\lean{PhysicalBKMContinuationCriterionFor u₀ ν}; \lean{KatoUniquenessOnExistenceTimes u₀ ν w}. Then:
(1) $T$ satisfies (C1), (C2), (C3); (2) $B=T$; (3) every Casimir is constant along $\omega(t)$;
(4) $T(\omega;H,M)=0$ for all $\omega,M$; (5) \lean{PhysicalVorticityCubicRate u₀ ν}, and there exist $u,p$ with
\lean{NS_PDE u p ν}, $u(0)=u_0$, $u(t)$ smooth and divergence-free for all $t\ge0$.
\end{theorem}

Conclusion (4) is the obstruction: under the very hypotheses of the capstone the tether supplies no
damping, so nothing in the geometric layer supports the hypotheses \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate} and
\lean{FrohmanianTetherClosingBound}, which carry the entire analytic conclusion (5). The capstone also
depends on Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:ke}, which we believe to be false (Section~\ref{sec:ke}); if it is, the
capstone is vacuous. No non-vacuity witness for the capstone is formalized.

\section{The periodic Clay statement and a conditional reduction}\label{sec:clay}

\lean{ClayStatements.Clay_B_exact} formalizes a version of Fefferman's statement (B) \cite{Fefferman2006}:
for every $\nu>0$ and every smooth, divergence-free, $\mathbb{Z}^3$-periodic $u_0$ on $\R^3$ there is a
solution $(u,p)$ that is $C^\infty$ on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, solves the equations with zero forcing, has
$u(0)=u_0$, and is periodic for all $t\ge0$. (Fefferman's formulation also contains a bounded-energy
condition; the formal statement is as quoted in Appendix~\ref{app:lean:clay}.)

\begin{theorem}[\lean{clay_B_exact_of_periodic_tether_bound}]
If \lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound ν} and \lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation ν} hold for every $\nu>0$, then
\lean{Clay_B_exact}.
\end{theorem}

\lean{PeriodicTetherClosingBound} is a uniform vorticity bound for all periodic smooth data and
\lean{PeriodicBKMContinuation} says that such a bound yields a global periodic smooth solution. Together they
are at least as strong as the conclusion; the reduction is a formal bookkeeping step and makes no progress
on (B).

\section{What the Lean development does and does not prove}\label{sec:does}

\paragraph{Proved (no further hypotheses).}
Symmetry of $T$ and (C3); antisymmetry of $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $\{H,H\}_{\mathrm{cl}}=0$;
Theorem~\ref{thm:obstr} ($B(H,M)=0$ for every symmetric $B$ satisfying (C2)); \lean{casimir_invariant} for
the concrete permutation group; the comparison Lemma~\ref{lem:cmp}; the scaling identity and the scale
non-invariance lemma; the local-maximum stretching bound \lean{stretching_inner_le_at_max} (given its strain
hypothesis \texttt{hCZ}); the zero-data facts and \lean{stepField_not_physical}; the Fourier-symbol identities
\lean{PlanSteps.bsSymbol_div}, \lean{PlanSteps.bsSymbol_curl} (mode-wise algebra, not connected to the
real-space Biot--Savart integral).

\paragraph{Proved as implications from named, open hypotheses.}
(C2) for $T$ (from Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:ke} and integrability); (C1) for $T$ (from \lean{SDiffActionHyp} and
\lean{TetherIntegralPreserved}); canonicity (from saturation and polarization); Casimir conservation (from
\lean{IsCoadjointMotion}); the conditional Jacobiator lemma; the conditional regularity theorems
\ref{thm:blue}, \ref{thm:cr}, \ref{thm:fft} and the capstone \ref{thm:capstone} (from rate or closing-bound
hypotheses, Kato existence, BKM continuation and uniqueness); the Clay (B) reduction.

\paragraph{Not proved, and not claimed.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Global existence, smoothness or uniqueness for 3D Navier--Stokes with large data; any part of the Clay
Millennium Problem.
\item The cubic rate \lean{FrohmanianTetherRate}, any closing bound, or any damping mechanism from the tether
(its damping coefficient is $0$ under (C2)).
\item Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:ke} ($\delta H/\delta\omega=u$), which we believe false as stated.
\item That the Euler flow is a coadjoint motion; transitivity on Casimir levels; \lean{TetherIntegralPreserved}.
\item A Jacobi identity for $\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\Tf}$, or a Poisson structure; the Jacobi identity for
$\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ (cited only).
\item Kato local existence \cite{Kato1984}, Beale--Kato--Majda \cite{BKM1984}, parabolic smoothing, energy
uniqueness: all are inputs, none is formalized.
\item Anything on the quotient torus: \lean{T3} is $\R^3$, and no diffeomorphism group of $\T$ is constructed.
\item Derivation of $T$ from (C1)--(C3): canonicity assumes diagonal agreement with $T$.
\end{itemize}
The physical motivations of earlier versions (fluid--gravity duality, Onsager's conjecture
\cite{Isett2018}, metriplectic viscous completion) are not formalized and play no role in any statement here.
